Most tradies understand their work as a physical system — the parts, the sequence, what connects where. Job management software is no different, just invisible. This exploded-view diagram pulls the ServiceM8 workflow apart so you can see each stage clearly, where it connects to the next, and where automation does the heavy lifting.

The solid line through the middle is the **job flow** — the path a job takes from first contact to repeat customer. The dashed lines show each stage pulled out from that flow. Tap any numbered part to see how the stage works, what to automate, and where most businesses slip up.

## How to Read This Diagram

- **The spine** is the central job flow. A job moves left to right: quote → schedule → on-site → invoice → Xero → review → follow-up.
- **The numbered modules** are the seven stages, pulled out so you can see their internal parts.
- **Each stage has three key parts** listed inside it — the moving pieces that make that stage work.
- **Tap a number** on the diagram to expand the matching explanation below.

## Where the Automation Actually Pays Off

The whole point of a workflow like this is that most of it runs without you. Four stages give the biggest return on setup time:

1. **Scheduling** — recurring jobs and saved job templates mean maintenance work books itself.
2. **Invoicing** — job line items become the invoice automatically; invoicing on-site cuts billing lag by days.
3. **Xero sync** — real-time, two-way, so you never reconcile in two systems.
4. **Reviews** — automated requests with a follow-up sequence turn more completed jobs into Google reviews.

The on-site stage is where compliance lives. Building your CCEW and CEC checklists as digital forms once — instead of paper — removes the re-keying step and keeps every job audit-ready.
