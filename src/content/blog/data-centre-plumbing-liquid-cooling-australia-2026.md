Data-centre pipefitters earn **$70,000–$120,000+** — and liquid cooling is the fastest-growing plumbing niche in Australia. The future of high-density AI compute is liquid: direct-to-chip and immersion cooling mean every AI rack now needs water, and that water is plumbed by pipefitters. This is not a threat to existing plumbing work. It is new work that barely existed five years ago — additive, not substitution.

Data centre plumbing is the pipefitting discipline that delivers and removes water for critical IT cooling: chilled water distribution loops, liquid cooling manifolds, condensate management and cooling-water treatment, governed by AS/NZS 3500 through NCC Volume 3.

The four largest hyperscalers — Microsoft, Amazon, Google and Meta — committed roughly **$700 billion in combined 2026 capital expenditure**, and Australia's 500+ MW pipeline (Western Sydney, Melbourne West, Brisbane) runs through 2030. Every megawatt of that pipeline needs water moved, treated and monitored — by plumbers and pipefitters who understand closed-loop systems.

This guide covers the five data centre plumbing workstreams, the skills and standards that gate the work, the real money in it, and how to position a plumbing business to capture it.

## The Play

Position your plumbing business as a data centre pipefitting contractor: add closed-loop hydronic capability to your licence base, learn water-treatment and leak-detection service work, and enter through maintenance scope — water testing, filter and strainer service, condensate programs — while the build phase is still running. The play works because liquid cooling is moving from niche to default for AI clusters, few plumbers hold the closed-loop and water-chemistry skills, and the maintenance contract runs for the facility's 20–30 year life — the install is the entry ticket, the water treatment is the annuity.

## The Opportunity: Five Data Centre Plumbing Workstreams

### Chilled Water Distribution Loops

Most Australian data centres reject heat through a chilled water loop: chillers produce 6–12°C water that circulates through precision cooling units, with condenser water rejecting heat through cooling towers or adiabatic plant. The loop is pipe-heavy — insulated chilled water mains, balancing valves, pumps, strainers, flow instrumentation — and every hall that commissions extends it.

For a plumbing business this splits into install scope (plant room pipework, loop fitout, commissioning with delta-T verification) and, more valuably, operation — flow balancing as halls come online in phases, strainer and filter maintenance, pressure and temperature verification. **Every new hall means repeat design-and-construct work on the same campus for years.**

### Liquid Cooling: Direct-to-Chip and Immersion

A standard server rack draws 5–10kW. AI GPU racks draw 30–100kW — and air cooling plateaus around 20–30kW per rack. That is why the highest-density AI halls are going liquid:

- **Direct-to-chip cold plates** — coolant manifolds and flexible hose runs deliver treated water or dielectric fluid to cold plates mounted on processors, with return lines carrying heat back to the loop
- **Rear-door heat exchangers** — rack-mounted coils that transfer heat from the server exhaust into a chilled water circuit
- **Immersion cooling** — entire racks submerged in dielectric fluid in sealed tanks, with circulation, filtration and heat-exchange plumbing around each tank

Every one of these systems is plumbed. The coolant distribution unit (CDU) — the manifold heart of a liquid-cooled hall — is a pipefitter's assembly: supply and return manifolds, isolation valves, flow meters, pressure gauges, and quick-connect hose drops to every rack. Australian hyperscale builds are speccing liquid-ready halls now, and the plumbers who can reference liquid cooling in 2026 will be quoting work in 2027 most of the industry cannot.

### Condensate Management

Precision cooling units dehumidify constantly, and every litre of condensate has to go somewhere — drain lines, condensate pumps, and containment that cannot back up into a live data hall. Condensate drain cleaning, pump replacement and containment checks are unglamorous, recurring, and **exactly the scheduled work that becomes a multi-year maintenance contract**. It is also a compliance issue: under **AS/NZS 3666**, cooling water and air-handling drainage systems must be maintained to prevent Legionella and biofilm growth, making condensate hygiene a billable obligation, not an optional extra.

### Water Treatment: Corrosion and Scaling Prevention

Closed-loop cooling water is not inert — it corrodes pipework, scales heat exchangers, and breeds biofilm unless it is chemically managed. Data centre water treatment covers inhibitor dosing, corrosion coupons, conductivity and pH monitoring, biocide programs and Legionella testing under **AS/NZS 3666**, plus closed-loop chemical treatment under **AS/NZS 3586** and cooling water treatment under **AS 2845.2**.

For a plumbing contractor this is the most attractive workstream in the list: it is **recurring, contracted, and chemistry-based** — a service a licensed plumber can own with training, and one that most residential and commercial plumbing businesses never touch. The water is already in your pipes; the treatment contract just keeps it healthy for the facility's entire life.

### Leak Detection and Containment

Liquid-cooled halls run water next to live electronics, so leak detection is a first-class requirement: flow and pressure monitoring on manifolds, humidity and moisture sensors under raised floors, drip trays and containment routing, and rapid isolation valve strategies. Pipefitters who can design, install and commission leak-containment systems — and respond to alarms — hold a specialist credential few competitors have. Backflow protection on potable connections is governed by **AS/NZS 2845.1**.

## What It Takes: The Data Centre Pipefitter's Toolkit

### Licensed Plumber + Pipefitting

Data centre plumbing work sits with licensed plumbers regulated by the state where the installation is physically located. In Victoria, the **Victorian Building Authority** administers plumbing work under the **Plumbing Act 1998 (Vic)**; in New South Wales, **NSW Fair Trading** licenses plumbers under the **Plumbing and Drainage Act 2011 (NSW)**; and in Queensland the **QBCC** holds the plumbing licence under the **QBCC Act 1991**. AS/NZS 3500 — Plumbing and drainage — is the technical baseline, adopted into law through **NCC Volume 3, the Plumbing Code of Australia**.

The premium part is the pipefitting. Closed-loop chilled water and liquid cooling work draws on hydronic skills — manifold assembly, valve selection, flange and grooved-pipe joining, pressure testing — closer to industrial pipefitting than residential plumbing. A licensed plumber with commercial hydronic experience is the ideal hire; a plumber who also reads P&ID-style loop drawings is gold.

### Closed-Loop Hydronic Systems

Data centre loops are closed systems: treated water circulates continuously at controlled pressure, temperature and flow, and the system must never drain for a routine service. That changes everything — isolation valves and bypasses are designed in, pressure testing follows manufacturer and AS/NZS 3500 protocols, and commissioning verifies flow rates and delta-T against design. Crews that understand closed-loop operation (as opposed to open residential systems) are worth materially more on site.

### Water-Chemistry Literacy

The differentiator between a data centre plumber and a general plumber is water chemistry. The basics every technician needs:

- **pH and conductivity** — what they indicate and how to read test results against treatment targets
- **Inhibitors and biocides** — what is dosed, when, and how to document it
- **Corrosion and scaling indicators** — coupon results, flow degradation, heat-exchanger fouling
- **Legionella risk management** — AS/NZS 3666 testing schedules and record-keeping

This is learnable — TAFE water-treatment short courses and supplier training (chemical suppliers run dosing and testing accreditation) get a crew operational quickly — and it converts a plumber into a compliance service provider.

### Critical-Environment Protocols

Finally, the protocols that separate data centre work from every other job site: N+1 redundancy means you never take a whole system down for service; change control means every intervention is logged, approved and reversible; and documentation means every reading, dose and test is recorded and archived for the life of the facility.

Plant-room work also falls under state WHS regulators — in New South Wales, **SafeWork NSW** enforces the **Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (NSW)**, which covers confined-space entry, working at height and permit-to-work systems in plant rooms. Data centre operators audit licences, logbooks and insurances before you set foot on site — public liability of **$20 million** is the standard expectation on critical infrastructure. |---|---|---|
| Residential plumbing callout | $90–$140/hr | Seasonal, competitive |

The contract values are where the structural advantage shows:

- **Install scope**: a chilled water loop or plant room fitout package for a mid-size plumbing contractor typically runs **$150,000–$500,000+**, often as a 12–24 month program of staged work as halls commission
- **Maintenance contracts**: water treatment, testing, strainer and filter service, condensate and leak-detection programs on a single campus typically land **$20,000–$60,000+ per year**, and multi-site hyperscale accounts run well beyond that
- **Recurring revenue tail**: a data centre campus needs water treatment and leak monitoring for its entire 20–30 year operating life — the install is the entry ticket, the treatment contract is the asset

The numbers vary with plant size and scope, but the structure does not: **a premium per hour on every job, plus a water-treatment annuity that outlasts the build.**

## How to Position Your Plumbing Business

### Step 1: The Upskilling Path

- Confirm every technician holds a current plumbing licence under your state regulator (VBA, NSW Fair Trading, QBCC, or your state equivalent)
- Get your team across **AS/NZS 3500** parts relevant to commercial hydronic work, and NCC Volume 3 — the Plumbing Code of Australia
- Send a senior tech through closed-loop hydronic commissioning training and a supplier-run water-treatment dosing and testing course
- Learn the liquid cooling basics: CDU manifolds, direct-to-chip loops, immersion tank circulation — vendor training (coolant and CDU manufacturers run courses) gets you credible fast

### Step 2: Build the Service Offering

Data centre clients buy capability, not goodwill. Package what you offer before you quote:

> **Data centre plumbing maintenance quote — scope template**
> 1. Closed-loop water testing and dosing per AS/NZS 3586 and AS/NZS 3666 (pH, conductivity, inhibitor levels, biocide)
> 2. Strainer, filter and Y-strainer service on chilled water loops
> 3. Condensate drain, pump and containment checks per AS/NZS 3666 microbial control
> 4. Leak detection system verification — flow/pressure alarms, moisture sensors, isolation valve testing
> 5. Backflow prevention device testing per AS/NZS 2845.1
> 6. Full critical-environment documentation pack for each visit

### Step 3: Partner With Electrical and HVAC Contractors

The fastest entry into data centre work is piggybacking on relationships that already exist. Electrical contractors already sub-contracting on data centre campuses need plumbing sub-contractors on the same packages — the fitout work is interleaved, and Tier 1 main contractors prefer packages that move together. Reach out to the electrical firms doing [data centre electrical work](/blog/ai-data-centre-growth-electricians-australia-2026), and the HVAC contractors doing [data centre cooling](/blog/ai-data-centre-cooling-hvac-contractors-australia-2026) — offer complementary plumbing and water-treatment capability on their next campus package, and register on the procurement portals (VendorPanel, AusTender) they already use. Three crews quoting complementary scope is a stronger bid than any one alone.

### Step 4: Quote the Maintenance Before the Build Ends

The contractors who own the water-treatment contracts on these campuses in 2030 are the ones quoting them in 2026, while the cranes are still up. Every install quote should carry a maintenance annex — use the scope template above. Data centre clients are unusually receptive to bundled install-plus-maintenance bids because they would rather contract water chemistry to the crew that plumbed the loop than manage a second vendor.

## Common Failure Modes

### Treating It Like Residential Plumbing

The number one failure: quoting a data centre like a house. The tolerance is tighter, the documentation is heavier, and the penalty for a service-window overrun is measured in dollars per second of downtime. Closed-loop water is not a hot water system — if your quote assumes residential testing and response times, you will lose margin on every job. Build the documentation hours in explicitly.

### Missing the Licensing and Insurance Gate

One unlicensed technician, or a public liability certificate below the **$20 million** threshold data centre operators expect, is enough to disqualify a sub-contractor from a panel permanently. Check your certificates against the [Electrical Contractor Insurance Australia 2026](/blog/electrical-contractor-insurance-australia-2026) thresholds before you bid — the same critical-infrastructure expectations apply to plumbing — and verify every tech's licence with your state regulator before they set foot on site.

### Treating Water Treatment as a Chore

Water chemistry is where the margin lives, but only if you run it as a contracted service with documented compliance. Crews that treat testing as a favour to the head contractor leave $20,000–$60,000+ per year per campus on the table — and hand the recurring revenue to a competitor who packages it properly.

### Waiting for the Job Ads

Tier 1 head contractors build their sub-contractor shortlists 6–12 months before work is tendered. If you see data centre plumbing jobs advertised on SEEK, the packages for that project were largely awarded months ago. Position now — licensing, water-treatment capability, partnerships — while the 2030 pipeline is still being planned.
