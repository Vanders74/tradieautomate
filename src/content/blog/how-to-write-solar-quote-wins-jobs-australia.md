## Quick-Reference: The Winning Solar Quote Checklist

Every quote you send should have all of these:

- ☐ Professional cover page with quote reference number
- ☐ Plain-English system summary (shareable with a partner, readable in 2 minutes)
- ☐ Design rationale specific to this customer's roof and usage
- ☐ Full equipment specifications with brands, models and warranties
- ☐ Financial analysis with Year 1 savings, payback period, and 25-year model
- ☐ Business credentials — CEC accreditation number, years trading, insurance confirmation
- ☐ 2–3 customer testimonials with first name and suburb
- ☐ Clear investment summary with deposit amount and next steps
- ☐ A follow-up plan: Day 1, Day 3, Day 7

Use this as your pre-send checklist before every quote leaves your inbox.

## What Customers Are Actually Asking When They Request a Quote

Before writing the quote, understand what the customer is really asking:

- *Can I trust this business?*
- *Will this system work for my house and situation?*
- *What will I actually save?*
- *What happens if something goes wrong?*
- *Is the price fair?*

Your quote needs to answer all five questions — not just provide a system specification and a price.

## The 7 Sections of a Winning Solar Quote

### Section 1: Professional Cover Page

First impressions. Your cover page should include:
- Your business name, logo, and contact details
- Customer name and property address
- Quote date and quote validity period (typically 30 days — STC values change quarterly)
- Quote reference number
- A clean, professional design that signals quality

A PDF quote with your branding, correct customer name, and a clean layout already puts you ahead of competitors sending a one-page price list in an email.

> **✏️ Copy-paste example — Cover Page intro line:**
>
> *"Solar Installation Quote — Prepared exclusively for [Customer Name], [Property Address]. Quote reference: TA-2026-[0001]. Valid until [Date + 30 days]. Questions? Call [Your Name] on [Phone]."*
>
> That one line — with a real reference number and expiry — signals professionalism and creates gentle urgency without being pushy.

---

### Section 2: System Summary (1 page, customer-readable)

Before the technical detail, give the customer a plain English summary they can share with a partner or read at 10pm when they're comparing quotes.

This section should be readable by someone with no technical knowledge. Keep it to one page or half a page.

> **✏️ Copy-paste example — System Summary paragraph:**
>
> *"Based on your annual electricity usage of 8,200kWh, we recommend a 10kW solar system with a 10kWh LFP battery. This system is designed to cover approximately 85% of your electricity needs and will generate around 14,600kWh of clean energy annually. Your estimated first-year savings are $2,340, with a simple payback period of 5.8 years. The system includes panels with a 25-year performance warranty and an inverter with a 10-year product warranty — all installed by our CEC-accredited team."*
>
> Write one of these for every quote. Customers who can understand their quote are more likely to accept it.

---

### Section 3: Why This System Was Recommended (the Design Rationale)

This section demonstrates that you've actually thought about their specific situation — not just quoted a standard package from a price list.

Include:
- Roof assessment summary (roof direction, pitch, shading observations, available space)
- Consumption analysis: what their current bill shows about usage patterns (morning peaks, evening peaks, what's driving usage)
- Why the specific system size was chosen (not just "it's our most popular size")
- Battery rationale (if included): how much self-consumption is currently happening, how the battery changes it
- Design assumptions: any assumptions made (typical occupancy hours, assumed feed-in tariff, expected grid tariff increases)

**This section is where you demonstrate E-E-A-T** — Expertise, Experience, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness. Both as a business and as a professional who has analysed their situation specifically.

> **✏️ Copy-paste example — Design Rationale opening:**
>
> *"Your home has a primary north-facing roof plane at approximately 22° pitch with no significant shading between 9am–4pm. Based on your most recent 12 months of bills, your average daily consumption is 22.5kWh, with a strong evening peak between 5–9pm — typical for a home with a pool pump and ducted air conditioning. This shaped our recommendation: the 10kWh battery is specifically sized to cover that 5–9pm peak from stored solar energy rather than drawing from the grid at peak tariff rates."*

---

### Section 4: Equipment Specifications

Customers who have done their research want to know what they're getting. Be specific:

**Solar panels:**
- Brand and model
- Wattage
- Quantity and total system kW DC
- Panel efficiency rating
- Product warranty (typically 25 years) and performance warranty

**Inverter:**
- Brand and model
- Type (string inverter, microinverters, optimisers + inverter)
- Rated output (kW AC)
- Product warranty (typically 10 years, with optional extension)

**Battery (if applicable):**
- Brand and model
- Usable capacity (kWh)
- Round-trip efficiency
- Warranty (cycles or years)
- Chemistry type (LFP recommended — include this, it's a trust signal)

**Mounting system:**
- Brand and type (rail, ballasted, ground mount)
- Wind loading compliance note
- Estimated roof penetrations/fixing method

**Monitoring:**
- Platform (app name, features)
- What the customer can see
- How you'll access for fault alerting (if you offer monitoring services)

> **✏️ Copy-paste example — Battery specification line:**
>
> *"Battery: BYD Battery-Box Premium HVS 10.2 | 10.2kWh usable | LFP chemistry (safer, longer cycle life than NMC) | 95% round-trip efficiency | 10-year / 4,000-cycle warranty | Integrates with Fronius GEN24 Plus inverter for seamless self-consumption management."*
>
> Include the chemistry type (LFP). Informed customers know LFP is safer and longer-lasting than older NMC batteries — naming it signals that you know your product.

### Section 5: Financial Analysis (The Most Important Section)

Most solar quotes show: system cost, STC discount, total price. That's not a financial analysis — it's a price tag.

> **Before you finalise this section:** Run the numbers through the [Solar Quote Profitability Calculator](/tools/solar-quote-calculator) to confirm your margin is where it needs to be. Once you know the job is profitable for you, present the financials in a way that makes it an easy yes for the customer.

A winning solar quote shows:

**Current electricity cost (annual):**
- Based on their last 12 months of bills or their stated average quarterly spend
- Broken into peak, off-peak, shoulder where relevant

**Year 1 savings calculation:**
- Energy generated (kWh, annualised from PVWatts, PVsell, or SolarEdge/Fronius design tool output)
- Self-consumption (% or kWh estimated from usage analysis)
- Grid import reduction
- Battery impact (if applicable)
- Estimated bill with solar vs. current bill
- Year 1 net saving in dollars

**Payback period:**
- Simple payback: total installed cost ÷ annual savings = years
- Note any assumptions (tariff increases, feed-in tariff, system degradation)

**Long-term financial model (10 and 25 years):**
- Cumulative savings over 10 and 25 years
- Net present value (optional, for financially sophisticated customers)

**Include a note on tariff assumptions:** If you've assumed electricity prices increase 3% per year, say so. Customers who understand your assumptions trust the model more.

> **✏️ Copy-paste example — Financial summary paragraph:**
>
> *"Based on your current electricity spend of $3,100/year and a system generating 14,600kWh annually, we estimate Year 1 savings of $2,340 — reducing your annual bill from $3,100 to approximately $760. At the installed price of $14,200 (after STC rebate), your simple payback period is 6.1 years. Over 25 years, the system is projected to generate $74,000 in cumulative savings, assuming a modest 3% annual electricity price increase. These calculations use SolarEdge output modelling for your specific roof and location."*

---

### Section 6: About Your Business (Credentials and Trust)

Include a short section establishing your credentials:
- CEC Accreditation number (and link to the CEC installer register)
- Years in business
- Number of installations completed
- Insurance summary (public liability and professional indemnity — confirm you hold them)
- Any industry memberships (NECA, Master Electricians Australia)
- Warranty and aftercare commitment: what happens if there's a fault, who they call, how quickly you respond
- 2–3 customer testimonials with first name and suburb

This section answers: *"Can I trust this business?"* It's not optional — it's often the deciding factor for customers comparing similar quotes at similar prices.

> **✏️ Copy-paste example — Credentials paragraph:**
>
> *"[Business Name] has completed over 340 solar and battery installations across [Region] since 2015. We hold CEC Accreditation #[number] — you can verify this on the Clean Energy Council installer register. We carry $20M public liability insurance and $5M professional indemnity cover. If anything goes wrong after installation, you call us directly on [phone] — not a manufacturer's helpline."*
>
> *"⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ — 'The quote was the most detailed we received by a mile. It was the reason we chose [Business Name].' — David M., Penrith"*

---

### Section 7: Investment Summary and Call to Action

The final page:
- Total system cost (before STC, with STC, after any other rebates)
- Payment options (upfront, solar loan, other financing if you offer it)
- Deposit amount required to proceed
- Expected installation date range
- What happens next (accept quote → site survey → installation → connection → monitoring)
- Your phone number, email, and an explicit request to call or email with questions

Make it easy to say yes. Include a "how to accept this quote" instruction.

> **✏️ Copy-paste example — Investment Summary and next steps:**
>
> *"Total system investment: $17,800 (before STC rebate of $3,600) = **$14,200 total installed price**.*
>
> *To proceed: a 10% deposit of $1,420 secures your installation date. Current lead time is 3–4 weeks. Once your deposit is received, we'll book a final site survey, submit your grid connection application, and confirm your installation date within 48 hours.*
>
> *Ready to go? Call [Name] on [phone], reply to this email, or click 'Accept Quote' in the email link above."*

## Solar Quote Template: A Structure to Adapt

Use this as your page-by-page blueprint. Most residential residential jobs fit cleanly into 8–9 pages.

| Page | Section | What to Include |
|------|---------|----------------|
| **1** | **Cover Page** | Business name & logo, customer name & address, quote ref #, date, validity (30 days), contact details |
| **2** | **System Summary** | Plain English overview: recommended system, annual generation, Year 1 savings, payback — shareable with a partner |
| **3** | **Your Situation** | Roof assessment, consumption analysis, design rationale — why *this* system for *this* home |
| **4–5** | **Equipment Specs** | Panels, inverter, battery, mounting, monitoring — brands, models, warranties, output ratings |
| **6–7** | **Financial Analysis** | Year 1 savings, simple payback, 10 & 25-year cumulative savings, tariff assumptions |
| **8** | **Why Choose Us** | CEC accreditation, years in business, install count, insurance, warranty promise, 2–3 testimonials |
| **9** | **Investment Summary** | Installed price (pre/post STC), deposit, payment options, installation timeline, next steps CTA |

> **Pro tip on length:** 8–10 pages is the residential sweet spot. More than 10 pages and most customers won't read it fully. Fewer than 7 usually means missing financial detail or credentials — the two sections that convert undecided customers.

## Sending the Quote: Timing and Format

**Speed wins.** Research consistently shows that solar quote conversion rates drop sharply with response time. Quotes sent within 4 hours of the enquiry convert at significantly higher rates than quotes sent the next day. Quotes sent on day 3+ are largely competing with quotes the customer has already begun evaluating.

**PDF via email is standard** but not optimal for conversion. Tools like [ServiceM8](https://www.servicem8.com/?ref=tradieautomate)'s quoting module, Proposify, or simPRO deliver quotes digitally with online acceptance, read-receipt tracking, and follow-up automation. If a customer reads your quote on their phone at 9pm, an automatic follow-up SMS the next morning is much more effective than hoping they remember to call you.

**Follow-up sequence:** Don't send and wait. A 3-touch follow-up sequence can convert 30–40% more quotes:

- **Day 1** (same day you send): Confirmation SMS — *"I've just sent your solar quote — happy to walk you through the numbers at a time that suits."*
- **Day 3**: Check-in call — ask if they have questions, not if they've made a decision.
- **Day 7**: Final follow-up — *"Just checking in before the quote expires. Is there anything I can clarify?"*

Most of your competitors don't follow up at all. This sequence alone is a competitive advantage.

## Common Solar Quote Mistakes That Lose Jobs

**1. No financial analysis — just a price**
"6.6kW system — $7,500 after STC" is a price. It's not a reason to buy. Show the savings.

**2. Generic system — no personalisation**
If your quote doesn't reference the customer's address, usage, or roof, it looks like a mail-merge template. It probably is.

**3. Sent too slowly**
If a customer has to follow up to get your quote, they've likely already received and evaluated a competitor's.

**4. No follow-up**
Most customers need 2–3 touches before making a decision. One quote and silence is a failed strategy.

**5. No credentials or trust signals**
An unsigned PDF from a business with no evident history is not reassuring for a $12,000–$20,000 purchase.

**6. Underselling the battery**
Many customers don't spontaneously ask for battery in the quote request but would say yes if shown the numbers compellingly. Always include a battery option alongside the solar-only price.

**7. Not checking your own margin**
Winning quotes that lose money on delivery is a trap. Run every quote through the [Solar Quote Profitability Calculator](/tools/solar-quote-calculator) before it goes out.

## FAQ

**How many pages should a solar quote be?**
For a residential solar installation, 8–10 pages is the right length. This gives you enough room for a proper financial analysis and credentials section without overwhelming the customer. Commercial quotes for larger systems typically run 12–16 pages.

**What's the difference between a solar quote and a solar proposal?**
In practice the terms are used interchangeably, but a *proposal* usually implies a more detailed document with financial modelling, multiple system options, and a formal project scope. A *quote* is typically a single recommended system with a price. Either way, the structure in this guide applies to both.

**How long should a solar quote be valid for?**
30 days is standard in Australia. STC (Small-scale Technology Certificate) values are set quarterly and can change meaningfully — a 90-day validity exposes you to pricing risk if the STC value drops. Always state your validity period and the reason (STC value changes) clearly in the quote.

**Should I include multiple system options in my quote?**
Yes — always include at least two options: a solar-only price and a solar + battery price. Customers who didn't ask for battery often say yes when they see the financial comparison side-by-side. Present the battery option as the recommendation, not the upsell.
