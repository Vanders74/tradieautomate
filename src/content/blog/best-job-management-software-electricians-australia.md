## What Electrical Job Management Software Has to Do

Generic business software doesn't cut it for electrical contractors. Here's what actually matters:

**Compliance documentation built in.** Electrical work in Australia carries strict documentation requirements — a certificate of compliance for every job, named and lodged differently in each state (CCEW via eCert in NSW, COES in Victoria, a certificate of testing and compliance in Queensland, eCoC in SA), plus test and inspection records and AS/NZS standards compliance. Your software needs to handle this, not just store photos.

**Mobile-first field experience.** Your team is on-site, not at a desk. The mobile app needs to be fast, reliable, and functional offline — because job sites don't always have reception.

**Quoting that works for electrical.** Electrical quotes often involve parts lists, labour rates, and material markups. The platform needs to handle this without requiring a workaround every time.

**Invoicing and payment speed.** Cash flow is everything in a trade business. Getting from job complete to invoice paid in under 24 hours requires automated invoicing, payment links, and accounting integration.

**Scheduling across multiple technicians.** As you grow beyond a sole trader, coordinating multiple techs — accounting for job duration, travel, and skill sets — becomes a major time sink without good tools.

## How Compliance Documentation Works

For Australian electricians, compliance documentation is non-negotiable. Here's how ServiceM8 handles it — and the tier limits that matter:

**Custom digital forms — Growing plan and above.** You build compliance checklists, test record templates, and inspection forms once. Field staff complete them on-site via the app. Every field is required before the job can be marked complete — no more missing paperwork discovered days later. This is a paid-tier feature: Free and Starter cannot build custom forms. On those plans your only option is buying a ready-made form from the ServiceM8 Form Store (which works on every plan), so if you're buying specifically for compliance paperwork, the real entry price is Growing at $79/mo.

**Photo requirements.** Jobs can be configured to require specific photos before sign-off — switchboard labels, installation photos, meter readings, before/after comparisons. Photos attach to the job record with a timestamp and the job association, which proves *when* — not *where*. Coordinates are a separate step: the free **Advanced Photo Branding** add-on can brand an emailed photo with GPS Location, and a marketplace add-on such as WorkM8 Photo GPS can embed coordinates back into the job diary, but both work only from the iOS app and the online dashboard — the Android Lite app has no add-on access.

**Certificate generation.** Certificate templates (including the Victorian COES layout) can be built as forms and auto-populated from job data — client details, property address, installation date, technician licence number. Generated PDFs are stored with the job record and can be emailed to the client automatically.

**The limit nobody mentions: a PDF is not a lodgement.** In NSW, a CCEW is not valid because you emailed a PDF. It must be submitted to Building Commission NSW through the **BCNSW eCert portal** (or an approved API) within **7 days** of the safety and compliance test — and from **1 July 2026 that portal is the only accepted channel**, with handwritten and PDF forms rejected. Building Commission NSW publishes an API so software can lodge directly; a form-builder that only produces a PDF still leaves you keying the certificate into eCert by hand. Victoria's COES and the other states' certificates each have their own lodgement channel too (see the state table below). When you evaluate any platform, ask whether it lodges, or only prints.

**Audit-ready records.** Every job in ServiceM8 has a complete, timestamped audit trail — who did what, when, using which forms, with what photos. When a licensing authority requests records for a random audit, they're available in minutes.

This is particularly valuable for electricians doing solar and battery work, where AS/NZS 5139 compliance requirements add another layer of documentation. Solar installers also need to manage STC creation and lodgement — the **Clean Energy Regulator** administers the Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme under the **Renewable Energy (Electricity) Act 2000**, and STC compliance documentation must be retained for audit purposes. See our [AS/NZS 5139 compliance guide](/blog/as-nzs-5139-battery-storage-compliance/) for specifics.

## State-by-State Compliance Considerations for Australian Electricians

One complexity that Australian electricians face — and that your job management software needs to accommodate — is the variation in compliance requirements by state and territory.

| State/Territory | Regulator | Certificate Type | Key Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| NSW | Building Commission NSW | Certificate of Compliance for Electrical Work (CCEW) | Under the Gas and Electricity (Consumer Safety) Act 2017. Must be lodged via the BCNSW eCert portal within **7 days** of the safety and compliance test; from 1 July 2026 eCert (or an approved API) is the only accepted channel — no PDFs, no carbon books |
| VIC | Energy Safe Victoria | Certificate of Electrical Safety (COES) | **Prescribed** work (switchboards, consumer mains, main earthing, solar and battery): certificate signed within **4 business days** of completing the work, electronic notification to ESV within **2 business days** of signing, independent Licensed Electrical Inspector inspection within **8 business days**, copies to the customer, ESV and the distribution business within **4 business days of the inspector returning the completed COES** (the inspector has 4 business days after the inspection to return it). **Non-prescribed** work: copy to the customer within one month |
| QLD | Electrical Safety Office (WorkSafe QLD) | Certificate of testing and compliance | Required for prescribed electrical work under the Electrical Safety Regulation 2026 (in force 1 September 2026, replacing the 2013 regulation) — it is not a "Form 7" |
| SA | Office of the Technical Regulator (within CBS) | electronic Certificate of Compliance (eCoC) | Required after **any** electrical work, not just notifiable work; prepared and lodged via ecoc.otr.sa.gov.au, with a maximum penalty under s61 of the Electricity Act 1996 of $50,000 for a body corporate or $10,000 otherwise, and an expiation fee of $315 |
| WA | Building and Energy (EnergySafety) | Electrical Safety Certificate | Under Electricity (Licensing) Regulations 1991 reg 52B, delivered within **28 days**, lodged via eNotice. Not required for maintenance work or like-for-like replacement |
| TAS | CBOS (Consumer, Building and Occupational Services) | Certificate of Electrical Compliance (CEC) | Lodged with TechSafe Australia within **3 days** of the work being energised, for notifiable electrical work. WorkSafe Tasmania handles WHS and licensing, not certificates |
| ACT | Access Canberra Electrical Inspectorate | Certificate of Electrical Safety (CES) | Lodged with Access Canberra; new installations cannot be energised until they pass an Access Canberra electrical audit |
| NT | NT WorkSafe (Electrical Safety Regulator) | Certificate of Compliance (CoC) | Under the Electrical Safety Act 2022 and Regulations 2024. Required for all electrical work except maintenance; provided within **20 business days** |

For electrical businesses operating across state lines — common for solar and battery installers following commercial contracts — managing these different requirements manually is a significant compliance risk. Note the deadlines alone: 3 days in Tasmania, 7 in NSW, 20 business days in the NT, 28 in WA, and in Victoria four separate clocks on prescribed work — 4 business days to sign the COES, 2 to notify ESV, 8 to get the inspection, and 4 more after the inspector returns the COES to distribute copies.

**What software can and can't do here:** you can build separate form templates for each jurisdiction, configured with the correct fields and certificate layouts. When a technician is doing a job in Victoria, they get the COES template; in Queensland, the certificate of testing and compliance template. That removes the data re-entry. It does **not** remove the lodgement step, because each state has its own portal — eCert in NSW, TechSafe in Tasmania, eNotice in WA, the OTR's eCoC system in SA. The strongest platforms are the ones that either integrate with those portals or export in the shape they need.
