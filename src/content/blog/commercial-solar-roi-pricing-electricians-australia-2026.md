The residential solar market in Australia is mature, crowded, and increasingly price-sensitive. System prices have compressed. Competition from national installers and volume dealers has tightened margins on the 6.6kW bread-and-butter install. Meanwhile, the commercial solar market — warehouses, factories, shopping centres, farms, strata complexes — is growing faster than the residential market, and the electricians and solar businesses moving into it are generating significantly more revenue per install-day.

This guide is the business case for Australian electricians considering the transition into commercial solar: what it earns, how to price it, what the compliance overhead looks like, and what the genuine ROI is for a solar business making the move.

---

## Why Commercial Solar Now?

Three forces are driving commercial solar demand in Australia in 2026:

**1. Energy cost pain is acute for businesses.** Commercial electricity rates in most Australian states are $0.25–$0.45/kWh for small-to-medium businesses. A 100kW solar system displacing 400MWh/year saves a business $100,000–$180,000 in electricity costs over 10 years. The payback period at current installed prices is 3–5 years. That's a compelling ROI for any business owner — and it makes your job as a solar installer relatively easy to sell.

**2. Grid exports are becoming restricted for residential — not for commercial.** As DNSP export limits tighten on residential networks (the CSIP-Aus 1.5kW export cap is now active across most states), large commercial systems with dedicated connection agreements are often able to export at full capacity or with negotiated limits. Commercial clients are less constrained.

**3. Government mandates and sustainability targets.** Many ASX-listed businesses, local councils, and state government agencies are under stakeholder pressure to reduce scope 2 emissions. Solar PV is the most cost-effective route. Corporate procurement teams have solar on their CapEx plans — they're looking for qualified commercial installers, not volume residential shops.

## Revenue Per Install-Day: The Commercial vs Residential Comparison

Before comparing pricing models, it's worth understanding the revenue efficiency difference between commercial and residential solar.

| Metric | Residential 6.6kW | Commercial 30kW | Commercial 100kW |
|---|---|---|---|
| Installed price | $7,000–$10,000 | $28,000–$42,000 | $90,000–$130,000 |
| Install time | 1 day (2 crew) | 2–3 days (3–4 crew) | 4–6 days (4–5 crew) |
| Revenue per install-day | $3,500–$5,000 | $7,000–$10,500 | $11,250–$18,600 |
| Compliance complexity | Low | Medium | High |
| Network approval required | Rarely | Often (30kW+) | Always |

The revenue-per-crew-day improvement is significant — typically 2–4x higher for commercial work. The compliance overhead increases too, but it's priced into the job; the real question is whether your team has the capacity to manage it.

## How to Price Commercial Solar Installations

Commercial solar pricing uses the same five-component model as residential, but each component scales differently.

### 1. Materials

Commercial equipment pricing follows a cost-plus model. Expect to pay:

- **Panels (tier-1 commercial-grade, 400–600W):** $0.28–$0.38/W supply cost, depending on volume and timing
- **String inverters (commercial-grade, 20–110kW):** $0.08–$0.14/W
- **Mounting systems (industrial racking, commercial roof):** $0.05–$0.10/W
- **Cabling, isolators, switchboard components:** $0.04–$0.08/W
- **Protection relay (required for 30kW+ on most DNSPs):** $1,500–$4,500 per project

**Total materials cost (100kW system, approximate):** $50,000–$70,000

Mark materials up at 12–18% to recover procurement overhead and carrying cost. Lower percentage than residential, but higher absolute dollar margin.

### 2. Labour

Commercial solar labour pricing must account for:
- Higher-skilled crew requirements (licensed electricians at higher award levels)
- Working at heights and elevated risk loading
- Longer install duration (larger systems, often more complex roof or switchboard configuration)
- Commissioning and DNSP inspection time

**Loaded cost for licensed electrician (commercial site):** $55–$70/hour
**Utilisation (commercial projects):** 80–85% (better than residential due to fewer small moves)
**Effective cost per billable hour:** $65–$85/hour

For a 100kW install with a 4-person crew over 5 days (160 crew-hours):
- Labour cost: 160 × $70 = $11,200
- Labour charge-out at $110/hour: 160 × $110 = **$17,600** (labour component of quote)

### 3. Compliance and Approvals

This is where commercial projects diverge sharply from residential:

| Compliance item | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| DNSP connection application | $500–$2,500 (DNSP charge, varies by network) |
| Protection relay supply and installation | $2,500–$5,500 |
| Engineering sign-off (100kW+) | $1,500–$4,000 |
| Metering installation (CT metering, three-phase) | $800–$2,500 |
| State electrical certificate (CES in VIC, or equivalent) | $200–$500 |
| AS/NZS 5033:2021 commissioning documentation | Staff time, $200–$600 in labour equivalent |

**Total compliance cost (100kW project):** $5,700–$15,600 depending on DNSP, state, and system complexity.

This is often underquoted. Build compliance costs as a line item in your quote — not as part of labour. It makes them visible to the client and protects your margin when approval timelines extend.

### 4. Overhead Allocation

Your overhead — office, vehicles, insurance, software, admin, marketing, finance costs — needs to be recovered across all revenue. A typical overhead rate for a solar business is 18–28% of labour cost, or $12–$18 per billable hour, depending on business size and complexity.

### 5. Margin Target

Commercial solar should target **15–22% net margin** after all costs. This is higher than what most residential installers achieve in practice (often 8–12% once compliance and call-back costs are included), but requires more careful job costing and DNSP risk management.

**Full pricing example — 100kW warehouse system:**

| Component | Cost | Quote line |
|---|---|---|
| Materials (panels, inverter, racking, wiring) | $62,000 | $72,000 (16% markup) |
| Labour (160 crew-hours @ $110) | $11,200 | $17,600 |
| Compliance & approvals | $9,500 | $9,500 |
| Overhead (on labour) | $2,800 | — (included in labour rate) |
| Total direct cost | $85,500 | — |
| Total quoted price (ex-STC discount) | — | **$99,100** |
| Less STC discount (approx. 100kW × 1.382 × zone derate × STC price) | — | −$12,800 |
| **Net customer price** | — | **~$86,300** |
| Net margin | **$13,600** | **~16%** |

## The STC Calculation for Commercial Systems

The Small-scale Technology Certificate (STC) mechanism works the same way for commercial systems under 100kW as it does for residential — reducing the customer price at point of sale, with certificates assigned to the installer and sold on the STC market.

**For systems over 100kW**, STCs are not available. Instead, the system is eligible for **Large-scale Generation Certificates (LGCs)** under the Large-scale Renewable Energy Target (LRET), administered by the **Clean Energy Regulator (CER)**. LGCs accrue over time based on actual generation — they're not a point-of-sale mechanism. For commercial clients, this means the upfront discount is smaller, but ongoing LGC revenue over the system life adds value.

**CEC accreditation** is required for all STC-eligible commercial installations. The Clean Energy Council (CEC) Installer Code of Conduct applies, and all grid-connected inverters must appear on the CEC's approved products list. Installation must comply with **AS/NZS 5033:2021** as a condition of CER audit eligibility.

## DNSP Engagement: The Variable That Breaks Commercial Solar Quotes

The single biggest risk in commercial solar pricing is underestimating DNSP (Distribution Network Service Provider) engagement time and cost.

For systems above certain thresholds — typically 30kW on a residential-class connection, but varying by DNSP and state — a formal network connection application is required before installation. The DNSP assesses the impact of the solar system on the local network, and may require:

- Protection relay installation (anti-islanding, over-voltage protection)
- Limitation of export capacity
- Metering upgrades (from basic interval to CT metering)
- In some cases, network augmentation at the customer's cost

**The risk:** DNSP approval can take 6–20 weeks. If a commercial client has planned around a specific installation date and the DNSP approval is delayed, your project cashflow, crew scheduling, and client relationship are all affected.

**How to manage it:**
- Lodge the DNSP application as soon as the customer signs — not when equipment arrives
- Include a DNSP approval condition in your contract (installation cannot commence until approval is received)
- Price the protection relay as a likely scope item, not a contingency — most commercial systems above 50kW on LV connections will need one
- Budget 10–15 weeks from contract signing to energisation for a 100kW commercial project

## What Does the Transition Actually Cost a Residential Solar Business?

Moving from primarily residential to commercial solar requires investment in several areas:

### Equipment and tooling

- Commercial-grade rooftop safety equipment (safety lines, anchor points) if not already held
- Larger cable drum infrastructure for commercial wiring runs
- Three-phase test equipment and commissioning tools
- Elevated work platform or scissor lift hire capability (or relationship with hire company)

**Estimated outlay:** $5,000–$20,000 depending on existing equipment inventory.

### Accreditation and training

- CEC large-scale design competency (if doing 100kW+ and taking on design responsibility): online course, approximately $800–$1,500
- Working at heights refresher (if crew cards have lapsed): $200–$400 per person
- DNSP-specific technical requirements: self-study, no direct cost

### Admin and quoting capability

Commercial quotes are more complex — materials lists are longer, DNSP fee schedules need to be incorporated, engineering costs need to be estimated. A structured quoting workflow and job management system is more important at commercial scale.

For the job management comparison across platforms that support commercial solar workflows, see our [best job management software for electricians guide](/blog/best-job-management-software-electricians-australia).

### Cashflow management

Commercial projects have different payment terms than residential. Large commercial clients often work on 30-day or 45-day payment terms, with progress payments at agreed milestones (design approval, materials delivery, practical completion). This requires more working capital than the same revenue in residential (where you typically get a deposit upfront and balance on installation day).

For a $100,000 commercial project with progress billing, you may have $60,000–$80,000 of materials and labour invested before the final payment arrives. Have a facility or credit arrangement in place before taking on your first large commercial project.

## Building a Commercial Solar Pipeline

Commercial solar leads don't come from the same channels as residential. A homeowner finds you via Google. A commercial client finds you via:

- **Trade associations and industry networks** (Master Electricians Australia, CEC member directory)
- **Commercial real estate agents and property managers** (who manage the properties where solar is being proposed)
- **Existing customers who also own commercial property**
- **Word-of-mouth from other businesses** (the owner of a factory talks to the owner of another factory)
- **Direct outreach to industrial precincts** (letterbox or email campaigns to commercial properties in solar-favourable areas)
- **Referrals from commercial energy brokers** (who advise businesses on energy costs and often recommend solar as part of that advice)

Building a commercial pipeline takes 6–12 months to develop. The lead times are longer (a commercial client may take 3–6 months from first contact to signed contract), but the contract values justify the investment.

## FAQ

**How many commercial solar installs does it take to replace the revenue from 50 residential installs per month?**

A 50-residential-install month at average $8,500 per system generates ~$425,000 per month in revenue. Equivalent commercial volume requires around 4–5 × 100kW installs per month at $90,000–$100,000 net price, or 12–15 × 30kW installs at $28,000–$35,000. The crew requirement drops significantly — 4–5 commercial installs can be delivered by a 5-person crew versus the 12–15 crew-days needed for 50 residential installs.

**Does commercial solar need a different contractor's licence?**

No additional licence type is required for commercial solar beyond standard electrical contractor and CEC installer accreditation. The complexity difference is in network connection obligations and design sign-off requirements (100kW+), not in licence classification. Check your state electrical regulator for any commercial site-specific requirements — some states have additional obligations for high-voltage work or embedded networks.

**What's the biggest hidden cost in commercial solar that catches businesses out?**

DNSP delays are the most common cashflow surprise — you've ordered equipment for a project and the DNSP approval takes three months longer than expected. The second biggest is protection relay scope creep: quoting without confirming the DNSP's protection requirements for the specific connection point. Get the DNSP's technical requirements in writing before finalising your quote on any system above 30kW.
