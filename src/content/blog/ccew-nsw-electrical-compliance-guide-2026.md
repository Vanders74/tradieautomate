It is created by the **Gas and Electricity (Consumer Safety) Act 2017** and the **Gas and Electricity (Consumer Safety) Regulation 2018**: s36 requires a safety and compliance test once the work is finished, and s37A requires the tester to lodge the certificate within 7 days. The CCEW itself is the legal record that the work has been tested and complies with AS/NZS 3000.

## What Is the CCEW? (2026 Requirements)

A Certificate of Compliance — Electrical Work (CCEW) is the certificate the licensed electrician who performed the safety and compliance test must lodge with Building Commission NSW. Since 1 July 2026, every CCEW must be lodged through the BCNSW eCert portal.

**Key CCEW concepts at a glance:**

1. **When CCEW is required** — Any work that installs, repairs, adds to or alters electrical equipment on an installation: solar, switchboard upgrades, EV chargers, air conditioning wiring, and also like-for-like replacement of water heaters, ovens, cooktops and accessories
2. **eCert portal** — Mandatory from 1 July 2026 — PDF, paper, NECA book and the Essential Energy portal are no longer accepted channels
3. **7-day deadline** — The CCEW must be lodged no later than 7 days after the safety and compliance test is completed
4. **Fees & penalties** — No government lodgement fee. On-the-spot fines up to $1,000 per occasion, and a maximum of 20 penalty units (individual) / 40 penalty units otherwise under s37A
5. **What the CCEW must include** — Installer and tester licence numbers, site address, work description, test results and the compliance declaration

For a step-by-step breakdown of each requirement, see the sections below.

The Certificate of Compliance — Electrical Work (CCEW) is a document that certifies:
1. That a safety and compliance test was carried out on specific electrical installation work, and by whom
2. That the work complies with the Gas and Electricity (Consumer Safety) Regulation 2018 and the Australian/New Zealand Wiring Rules (AS/NZS 3000, plus AS/NZS 5033 for solar and AS/NZS 5139 for batteries)
3. The details of the work performed, the installation address, and the licence numbers of the installer and tester

From 1 July 2026, all CCEWs must be submitted through the **BCNSW eCert portal** — the mandatory digital lodgement system. Handwritten forms, PDF forms, and previous submission methods are no longer accepted. The BCNSW eCert portal is the only valid submission channel.

## When Is a CCEW Required in NSW?

Building Commission NSW's own test, restated in industry advisory note 2/2026 (issued August 2026, updated September 2026), is deliberately broad: **a CCEW must be submitted for any type of electrical work that involves installing, repairing, adding or altering electrical equipment on an electrical installation.**

What that covers in practice:

**Residential and commercial electrical installation:**
- Installing or altering fixed electrical wiring
- Installing or modifying switchboards
- Connecting fixed electrical equipment to the wiring system
- **Like-for-like replacement** of water heaters, ovens, cooktops and electrical accessories

**Solar and battery installations:**
- Installation of any solar PV system connected to the building wiring
- Installation of battery energy storage systems connected to the building wiring
- Connection of an EV charger (EVSE) as a fixed electrical installation

**On replacement work specifically:** NSW does *not* exempt like-for-like swaps. The regulator named water heaters, ovens, cooktops and electrical accessories as examples of work that requires a CCEW. If you have been treating "same unit, same wiring" as paperwork-free, that is the single most likely place your business is exposed.

**The one thing that must happen first:** an installation, or part of one, must not be energised unless a safety and compliance test has established its safe operation and compliance with AS/NZS 3000 (Regulation s34(4)). The CCEW is the record of that test — so it follows the test, not the invoice.

## Who Can Lodge a CCEW in NSW?

The obligation sits on **the tester** — the qualified person who conducted the safety and compliance test. Under the Regulation, "qualified person" means a person authorised under the Home Building Act 1989 to do electrical wiring work without supervision: an individual contractor licence holder or a qualified supervisor certificate holder.

That produces a rule most guides get backwards. It is **not** "the business lodges, the employee can't":

- **The individual lodges.** BCNSW eCert registration accepts an individual contractor licence or a qualified supervisor certificate only. The portal explicitly rejects a company contractor licence, partnership contractor licence, tradesperson certificate or provisional tradesperson certificate.
- **A company cannot lodge in its own name.** Company and partnership contractor licences exist so the business can contract and advertise — they are not eCert logins. The nominated qualified supervisor is the person who lodges.
- **Employed electricians do lodge.** If you are the qualified supervisor who ran the test inside a licensed company, the certificate goes in under your individual licence and your name.

For solar businesses using subcontractors, that last point is the one to get right: the subcontractor who tests their own work lodges their own CCEW. If you are the head contractor, your obligation is to confirm they have lodged — not to lodge on their behalf.

## CCEW Deadline, Fees & Penalties (2026)

The CCEW must be lodged with Building Commission NSW — via BCNSW eCert or an approved API — **no later than 7 days after the safety and compliance test is completed** (Regulation s37A). Not 7 days after the job is invoiced, not 7 days after the office catches up. The clock starts when the test finishes. For a detailed breakdown of exactly when that clock starts, see our [CCEW 7-day deadline rule guide](/blog/ccew-nsw-7-day-deadline-rule/).

Best practice is to lodge within 24–48 hours of the test — while the installation details are fresh, photos are uploaded, and documentation is complete. Waiting until day 6 creates unnecessary risk.

**There is no government lodgement fee.** eCert lodgement costs nothing, and the NSW pages list no charge for submitting a CCEW. What a CCEW costs you is time — and, if you miss the deadline, this:

- **On-the-spot fines of up to $1,000** for each occasion a CCEW is not supplied to Building Commission NSW
- **s37A maximum penalty of 20 penalty units** for an individual (about $2,200 at the NSW penalty unit value of $110) and **40 penalty units** otherwise (about $4,400)
- **Up to $55,000 for an individual and $550,000 for a corporation** for failure to carry out electrical installation work in accordance with the technical standards in the Regulation (Gas and Electricity (Consumer Safety) Act 2017, s34) — the much larger number, and the one attached to the work itself rather than the paperwork. Guides that quote "$550,000" on its own are quoting the corporate maximum at a sole trader.

One clarification, because this figure is widely miscopied: **$22,000 for an individual and $110,000 for a corporation is the Home Building Act 1989 penalty scale for unlicensed contracting** (200 and 1,000 penalty units). It is not the CCEW lodgement penalty, and it is not the number that applies if you lodge late.

**Two separate obligations for solar installations:**
1. **CCEW** — lodged with Building Commission NSW within **7 days** of completing the safety and compliance test
2. **Network notification** — submitted to the relevant DNSP (Ausgrid, Endeavour Energy, or Essential Energy) as part of the connection application

These are independent requirements to separate bodies. Lodging the CCEW does not satisfy the network notification obligation, and vice versa. Many electricians confuse the two — don't be one of them.

## What Must the CCEW Include?

A compliant NSW CCEW must include:

**Installer and tester information:**
- Name of the licensed electrician who did the work (installer) and who tested it (tester)
- Individual licence or qualified supervisor certificate numbers for each
- Business name of the contracting entity, where applicable
- Contact details

**Installation information:**
- Full address of the installation where work was performed
- Name of the property owner (or occupier where different)

**Work description:**
- Detailed description of the electrical installation work performed
- For solar: panel brand/model, number, inverter brand/model, system capacity (kW), installation location on property
- For battery: battery brand/model, capacity (kWh), installation location
- Date the work and the test were completed

**Compliance declaration:**
- Declaration that the work complies with the Gas and Electricity (Consumer Safety) Regulation 2018 and the Australian/New Zealand Wiring Rules
- Test results recorded against the s36 checks — earthing continuity, insulation resistance, polarity, circuit connections — plus RCD testing where devices were installed or replaced
- Date of lodgement

## BCNSW eCert Portal — Mandatory Digital CCEW Submission

**As of 1 July 2026, the BCNSW eCert portal is the only valid submission channel for all CCEWs in NSW.** This is a hard regulatory change — not a preference.

The following submission methods are **no longer accepted**:
- CCEW PDF forms (the old downloadable form)
- Essential Energy portal lodgements
- Hard copy forms, including NECA book forms

**What this means for your business:**
1. If you haven't registered a BCNSW eCert account yet, you cannot lodge CCEWs legally after 1 July 2026
2. A CCEW sent by any retired method is treated as not lodged — penalties apply
3. The portal records the certificate with Building Commission NSW and emails copies out based on what you enter: the customer (if their email is included), the distributor where required, and your employer or principal contractor (if their email is included)

Registration is free. The portal is at [ecert.buildingcommission.nsw.gov.au](https://ecert.buildingcommission.nsw.gov.au/). What you need:

- An **individual contractor licence or qualified supervisor certificate** — company, partnership and tradesperson licences are rejected at sign-up
- Your name entered exactly as it appears on that licence or certificate
- A valid email address, plus two-factor authentication on every login (one-time passcode, valid 10 minutes)

**No Service NSW account is needed**, and there is no contractor-licence-plus-Service-NSW linkage step — a lot of guides repeat that and it sends people down the wrong path.

**For compliance-heavy electrical businesses:** If you lodge 10+ CCEWs per month, the portal's pre-fill and history features will save hours over the old manual methods. The mandatory transition is actually a workflow improvement — but only if you register and learn the system before you need it for a live job.

> **Working across NSW and VIC?** Victoria's equivalent system is ESV Connect for COES lodgement. See our [COES Victoria complete guide](/blog/certificate-electrical-safety-ces-victoria-guide-2026/) for the side-by-side comparison of both state portals.

### How to Lodge a CCEW via BCNSW eCert

1. Register at [ecert.buildingcommission.nsw.gov.au](https://ecert.buildingcommission.nsw.gov.au/) with your individual licence or qualified supervisor certificate number (free)
2. Log in with your one-time passcode and select the **Electrical work** tile
3. Confirm whether it is new work, or rectification of work under a written direction notice (WDN)
4. Complete the sections that apply — installation details, the work performed, test results and declaration. Select **Save and continue** as you go: entries are lost if you close or refresh the browser
5. Submit — the portal records the certificate with Building Commission NSW and emails you a PDF copy
6. File the certificate number against the job record and retain the PDF

### Lodging from job software — what the approved API actually covers

The portal is not the only channel, but it is the only *default* one. Building Commission NSW runs a self-service API that lets eligible businesses and job-management vendors submit CCEW data straight from their system into eCert. It is worth knowing exactly what it does, because "integrated with eCert" is becoming a sales line:

- **Eligibility is gated and onboarding is manual.** Access is limited to eligible entities — job-management software vendors, and electrical businesses holding a company or partnership contractor licence that submit high volume — who have been onboarded by BCNSW. There is no public list of approved products, so ask the vendor for their Agency ID, not a screenshot.
- **Rectification CCEWs are excluded.** A rectification certificate (work done under a written direction notice) cannot be submitted through an integrated system at all — it has to be lodged directly in the eCert portal.
- **The API moves the paperwork, not the responsibility.** The individual electrician stays legally responsible for the accuracy and completeness of every CCEW submitted under their licence, and eCert remains the system of record. The business using the API must be able to give BCNSW the CCEW information within 7 days of receiving it from the electrician.
- **A form builder is not an integration.** Software that produces a PDF still leaves you keying the certificate into eCert by hand. If a vendor says it "lodges your CCEW", ask which API and whether they are onboarded.

## CCEW and Solar — Specific Requirements

For solar PV installation in NSW, the CCEW obligation exists alongside several other compliance requirements that operate separately:

### Network notification

Before connecting a solar system to the grid, the relevant distribution network service provider (DNSP) must be notified:
- **Ausgrid territory (Sydney, Central Coast, Hunter):** Ausgrid connection application portal
- **Endeavour Energy territory (greater western Sydney, Blue Mountains, Wollongong):** Endeavour Energy portal
- **Essential Energy territory (regional/rural NSW):** Essential Energy portal

Network notification is required before connection, not after — it's not a post-installation compliance item.

### From November 2026: the NSW CER Installer Portal

The biggest change to NSW solar compliance since eCert, and almost no guide covers it yet.

From **2 November 2026**, new and upgrading consumer energy resources (CER) systems up to 200kW that have a DNSP connection application submitted and approved from that date must be commissioned through the **NSW Consumer Energy Resources Installer Portal**. Ausgrid's start date is **23 November 2026**.

For those jobs, the portal replaces the separate CCEW lodgement in eCert and the AEMO Distributed Energy Resources Register (DERR) submission — both are captured in the one commissioning process. There is no installer fee to register or submit through the CER portal, though DNSP application fees still apply.

Three things to watch:
- If the job also includes electrical work outside the CER scope, you still lodge a **separate CCEW through BCNSW eCert** for that work — the portal only replaces the CCEW for the CER work itself.
- **From November 2026 the inverter requirement changes too:** new and upgrading rooftop systems up to 200kW must be fitted with inverters compliant with CSIP-AUS (Common Smart Inverter Profile — Australia), and the portal tests that each newly registered system is actually communicating with the DNSP's utility server over the internet. Where a site has no reliable connection, a low default export limit applies until it does. That is a quoting problem as much as a compliance one: a compliant CSIP-AUS inverter and a working data connection are now part of the job, not an optional extra.
- The Emergency Backstop Mechanism (EBM) enablement rolls out in phases per network, managed by each DNSP.

### AS/NZS 5033 installation records

Your installation must comply with AS/NZS 5033:2021 (the solar PV installation standard). Documentation of compliance — string sizing calculations, labelling records, installation photos — should be retained even though they don't form part of the CCEW submission.

### AS/NZS 5139 for battery

Battery installations require compliance with AS/NZS 5139:2019. The battery clearance compliance, ventilation assessment, and commissioning records should be documented and retained alongside the CCEW.

See our [solar compliance checklist](/blog/solar-compliance-checklist-2026/) and [AS/NZS 5139 guide](/blog/as-nzs-5139-battery-storage-compliance/) for the complete compliance picture.

## Common CCEW Audit Failures — What Gets Contractors Caught in 2026

Building Commission NSW's Electrical Compliance Team receives complaints and runs audits. These are the failure patterns that consistently appear in compliance outcomes:

**1. Missing the 7-day window**
The most common breach — and the easiest to prevent. The clock starts when the safety and compliance test is completed, not when you send the invoice. Set a job management reminder at the point of sign-off. Contractors who batch CCEW lodgement at the end of the week regularly miss the window when jobs finish on a Friday before a public holiday.

**2. Vague or incomplete work description**
"Electrical installation" or "solar installation" is not sufficient. A valid CCEW work description must be specific enough for an auditor to understand exactly what was installed and where. For solar: panel count, panel brand and model, inverter brand and model, total system size in kW, whether there's a battery, battery brand and kWh rating, and the type of grid connection. For switchboard work: circuit count, breaker specifications, RCD types installed.

**3. Wrong name or licence number**
The eCert form checks your licence as you enter it, so the classic failure here is the named individual: the installer (who did the work) and the tester (who tested it) may be two different people, each with their own licence number. Entering the contractor's business name in the licence field is not a valid entry — the field wants an individual licence or qualified supervisor certificate number.

**4. Confusing CCEW with DNSP notification**
Lodging the CCEW satisfies the Building Commission NSW obligation. It does not submit the network connection application to the DNSP (Ausgrid, Endeavour Energy, or Essential Energy). These are separate obligations to separate bodies. Many contractors make this error and receive complaints from DNSPs about unnotified connections.

**5. Customer never receives a copy**
There is no separate statutory "give the owner a copy" step in eCert — but if you don't enter the customer's email when you lodge, no copy goes to them automatically, and the homeowner is left with no record. When they sell or refinance and can't produce a CCEW for the installation, they will trace it back to you.

**6. Lodging before the test is complete**
A CCEW certifies that the safety and compliance test has been carried out and the work complies. Lodging one before all punchlist items are done is a false certification — potentially exposing you to disciplinary action and voiding insurance coverage.

**7. Poor photo documentation for solar**
While photos aren't part of the CCEW itself, Building Commission NSW and DNSPs increasingly cross-reference CCEW records against installation photos during solar audits. A CCEW with no corresponding job documentation is a red flag. Geo-tagged, timestamped photos should be stored against every job.

> **Working across state lines?** Victoria issues a COES, not a CCEW — and the clock is far tighter than the "one month" most guides quote. For prescribed work (switchboards, consumer mains, main earthing, solar, battery) the certificate must be signed within 4 business days of completing the work, ESV notified within 2 business days of signing, an independent LEI inspection done within 8 business days, and copies issued within 4 business days of the inspector returning the completed COES. The one-month window applies only to non-prescribed work. Different regulator too — Energy Safe Victoria. See our [Victoria COES complete guide](/blog/certificate-electrical-safety-ces-victoria-guide-2026/) for the side-by-side comparison — getting this wrong when your crews cross the Murray is a material compliance risk.

## Consequences of Non-Compliance (2026 Penalties)

**Lodgement penalties (s37A):** The Regulation sets the maximum at 20 penalty units for an individual and 40 penalty units otherwise. At the NSW penalty unit value of $110 that is roughly **$2,200 and $4,400** — per certificate, not per year. Building Commission NSW also issues **on-the-spot fines of up to $1,000** for each occasion a CCEW is not supplied.

**The big number:** failure to carry out electrical installation work in accordance with the technical standards in the Regulation attracts **up to $55,000 for an individual and $550,000 for a corporation** (Act s34). That is the exposure that follows bad work, not late paperwork — and the $550,000 figure you see quoted everywhere is the corporate one.

**Licence risk:** Repeated or serious non-compliance can result in licence conditions or suspension — and since eCert lodgement is tied to your individual licence, a pattern of missed or defective certificates shows up against your name, not just the business's.

**Insurance exposure:** Work performed without a CCEW may create issues with professional indemnity insurance coverage in the event of an incident.

**Property owner disputes:** A homeowner attempting to sell or refinance a property may discover no CCEW on record for an electrical installation — creating a dispute with the installing contractor.

It must be lodged no later than 7 days after the test is completed.

### Who can issue a CCEW in NSW?
The tester — a qualified person authorised under the Home Building Act 1989 to do electrical wiring work without supervision — must lodge the CCEW. In practice that means the individual contractor licence or qualified supervisor certificate holder who performed the safety and compliance test. BCNSW eCert does not accept business licences at all: you cannot register a company or partnership contractor licence to lodge certificates.

### What happens if you don't lodge a CCEW within 7 days in NSW?
Lodging late or not at all is an offence under s37A of the Gas and Electricity (Consumer Safety) Regulation 2018. The maximum penalty is 20 penalty units for an individual (about $2,200 at the NSW penalty unit value of $110) and 40 penalty units otherwise. Building Commission NSW also issues on-the-spot fines of up to $1,000 for each occasion a CCEW is not supplied. In practice, electricians breach this most often on large multi-job weeks where paperwork piles up — a job management trigger at the point of sign-off prevents it.

### Does a CCEW need to be issued for solar installations in NSW?
Yes. Any solar PV installation that involves electrical connection work requires a CCEW in NSW. This includes the AC wiring from the inverter to the switchboard, any switchboard modifications, and the grid connection. From 2 November 2026 (23 November for Ausgrid), new and upgrading CER systems up to 200kW whose DNSP connection application is submitted and approved from that date must be commissioned through the NSW CER Installer Portal, which replaces the separate eCert CCEW lodgement and the AEMO DERR submission for that work. In addition to the CCEW, solar installers must lodge STC documentation with the Clean Energy Regulator and hold Solar Accreditation Australia (SAA) accreditation — the Clean Energy Council has not accredited installers since 29 May 2024.

### What information must be included on a NSW CCEW?
A valid NSW CCEW records the installer and tester details (names and individual licence or supervisor certificate numbers), the installation address, the work performed, the safety and compliance test results, and the declaration. For solar it also records array and inverter details; for battery, the storage system details. The BCNSW eCert form only asks you to complete the sections that apply to the work you are certifying — incomplete certificates are a common trigger for compliance action.
