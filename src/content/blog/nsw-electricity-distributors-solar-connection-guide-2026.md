Connecting solar in New South Wales means working with one of three electricity distributors — **Ausgrid, Endeavour Energy, or Essential Energy** — and getting the *right one* first, because their export limits and application portals are different. Lodge an application with the wrong distributor, or assume a 10kW export limit that's actually 5kW in your area, and the job stalls before it starts.

**NSW has three electricity distributors**, each covering a distinct part of the state: **Ausgrid** (northern Sydney, Central Coast, Hunter), **Endeavour Energy** (western Sydney, Blue Mountains, Southern Highlands, Illawarra), and **Essential Energy** (regional and rural NSW). The distributor — not your retailer — approves the connection and sets the export limit. Unlike South Australia's single-distributor setup, NSW installers must identify the correct distributor before lodging any solar, battery, or EV charger application.

This guide covers all three distributors: their coverage, their different export limits, the application process, flexible exports (still rolling out), CCEW requirements, and the mistakes that get applications knocked back.

## Who Covers What: The NSW Distributor Map

NSW's grid is a three-tier structure, and the distributor layer is where your connection gets approved.

| Layer | Company | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| Transmission | Transgrid | High-voltage network |
| **Distribution** | **Ausgrid / Endeavour / Essential** | Poles, wires, substations — **approve connections and set export limits** |
| Retailer | AGL, Origin, EnergyAustralia, etc. | Billing, feed-in tariff — separate from the distributor |

**Distributor coverage lookup:**

| Area | Distributor |
|---|---|
| Sydney CBD, North Sydney, Northern Beaches | Ausgrid |
| Central Coast, Newcastle, Hunter | Ausgrid |
| Parramatta, Penrith, Western Sydney | Endeavour Energy |
| Blue Mountains, Southern Highlands | Endeavour Energy |
| Wollongong, Illawarra | Endeavour Energy |
| Dubbo, Wagga Wagga, Tamworth, Albury | Essential Energy |
| Regional & rural NSW | Essential Energy |

**How to confirm:** the customer's electricity bill names their distributor, and every distributor has an online postcode/suburb lookup. Don't guess — a wrong-distributor application is rejected and costs days.

## Export Limits: The NSW Differentiator

This is the section that matters most for quoting, because **NSW export limits are not uniform** — they change by distributor and by phase.

| Distributor | Single-phase inverter / export | Three-phase inverter / export | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ausgrid** | 10kW / **10kW** | 30kW / **30kW** | May allow battery inverters to exceed the overall inverter limit |
| **Endeavour Energy** | 10kW / **5kW** | 30kW / **30kW** | Battery inverters do **not** count toward the phase limit |
| **Essential Energy** | 10kW / **5kW** | 15kW / **5kW** | Rural areas drop to **3kW** per phase |

**The practical upshot:** a 10kW solar system on a single-phase Endeavour Energy or Essential Energy connection exports only 5kW — half its capacity — while the same system on Ausgrid exports the full 10kW. Getting this right at the quote stage prevents an expensive re-quote and an unhappy customer who was promised export they can't actually send.

Export limits are one of the fastest-moving parts of this space — always confirm the current figure in the distributor's portal or connection guidelines before quoting, not from memory.

## Flexible Exports: Coming to NSW, But Not Here Yet

Unlike South Australia (where flexible exports have been mandatory since 1 July 2023), **NSW is still rolling them out.**

- **Endeavour Energy** is phasing flexible exports in from **late 2026**, making it the default connection offer for new and upgraded solar systems. Under flexible exports, single-phase customers can export up to **10kW most of the time**, dynamically adjusted.
- The **NSW Government's CER Installer Portal** will require new and upgraded rooftop solar systems up to **200kW** to use **CSIP-AUS** compliant inverters as part of this staged rollout.
- Until then, fixed export limits apply across all three networks.

**What this means for installers:** for now, quote to the fixed limits above. But design systems that are *flexible-exports-ready* — a **CSIP-AUS compliant, internet-connected inverter** (see our [CSIP-AUS explainer](/blog/csip-aus-export-limit-1-5kw-solar)) — so the customer can take advantage of higher exports when the rollout reaches their area. That's a genuine differentiator in your quote.

Inverters must also meet **AS/NZS 4777.1:2024** (installation) and **AS/NZS 4777.2:2024** (inverter product requirements), and be on the Clean Energy Council's approved products list.

## The Solar Connection Process

The exact steps vary slightly by distributor, but the shape is consistent across NSW:

1. **Confirm the distributor** — via the customer's bill or a suburb lookup.
2. **Lodge the application** — through the distributor's portal (Essential Connections for Essential Energy; Ausgrid and Endeavour have their own online applications).
3. **Receive approval** — small systems auto-approve; larger ones go to technical review. Essential Energy auto-approves ≤3kW rural / ≤5kW urban and reviews larger systems (typically 4–8 weeks).
4. **Install per the approved documents** — the Voltage Rise Calculation and Single Line Diagram you submitted are binding; a deviation requires a new application.
5. **Commission + register** — the inverter, plus device registration where flexible exports apply.
6. **Submit the CCEW** — the NSW-specific compliance certificate (see below).
7. **Close out** — complete the connection in the portal.

The documents matter more in NSW than in some states: Essential Energy explicitly requires an accurate **Voltage Rise Calculation** and **Single Line Diagram** with the application, and incomplete or inaccurate applications are declined.

## Battery Storage Requirements

Battery connections follow the same distributor framework as solar, plus the battery-specific compliance layer:

- Battery installations must meet **AS/NZS 5139** — see the [AS/NZS 5139 compliance guide](/blog/as-nzs-5139-battery-storage-compliance).
- Whether the battery inverter counts toward the phase inverter limit depends on the distributor (see the table above — it's a real quoting difference).
- STC eligibility for the solar component is governed by the **Clean Energy Regulator** under the **Renewable Energy (Electricity) Act 2000** — see the [STC claim process](/blog/stc-claim-process-solar-installers-australia).

## New Connections, Service Upgrades & Switchboards

Beyond solar, the same distributors handle the full connection range:

- **New homes and subdivisions** — new connections, temporary builder's supplies
- **Service upgrades** — consumer mains, switchboard, and three-phase upgrades
- **Capacity increases** — when demand (EV chargers, heat pumps, growth) outgrows the existing supply

The discipline is identical to solar: confirm the network constraints and distributor requirements *before* quoting, because an upgrade that needs a line or transformer study is a different job to a switchboard swap.

## Approval Timeframes

| Application type | Typical timeframe |
|---|---|
| Essential Energy ≤3kW rural / ≤5kW urban | Auto-approval |
| Essential Energy larger systems | 4–8 weeks (technical review) |
| Ausgrid / Endeavour metro small systems | Portal-dependent, often days |
| Larger / regional systems | Site-specific, longer |
| Systems under the Emergency Backstop | Install + commission by 31 Dec 2026 |

**Note:** Ausgrid and Endeavour Energy have advised that systems approved under current arrangements must be installed and commissioned by **31 December 2026** under the NSW Emergency Backstop Mechanism. Check the current backstop requirements before quoting a system that may not be installed in time.

## Common Mistakes That Get Applications Knocked Back

- **Wrong distributor** — lodging with the wrong network is an instant rejection; confirm via the customer's bill.
- **Incomplete or inaccurate applications** — missing NMI, meter number, or an inaccurate Voltage Rise Calculation / Single Line Diagram (a known Essential Energy decline reason).
- **Wrong export limit assumption** — assuming 10kW on a 5kW-export Endeavour or Essential single-phase connection.
- **Non-compliant inverter** — not on the CEC approved list, or not meeting AS/NZS 4777:2024.
- **Missing CCEW** — the NSW compliance certificate is separate from the connection approval.
- **Deviation from the approved SLD** — installing something different to the approved diagram requires a new application, not a note.
