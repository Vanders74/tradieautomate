Every Victorian Registered Electrical Contractor knows the 28-day CES rule. Lodge the Certificate of Electrical Safety within 28 days of completing the work, or face the penalties.

But here's what most RECs miss: ESV doesn't just check whether you lodged. They check **what** you lodged, **when** you lodged it, and — in the 2026 audit cycle — **whether your lodgement pattern matches your actual job records**.

A single CES failure might cost you $150–$300 in late fees. A **pattern** of failures — even innocently missed certificates — triggers an audit that can cost $15k–$30k in penalties and months of compliance stress.

## The Gap Nobody Talks About

Most RECs discover they have CES compliance gaps the same way: ESV sends a notice, the REC scrambles to find certificates from 18 months ago, and by then the lodgement window is so far past that the late fee is the least of the problem.

The gap isn't malicious — it's administrative. A certificate forgotten because the job finished on a Friday. A solar install where the admin assumed the CEC paperwork was the CES. An EV charger installation where nobody made the connection between the electrical work and the certificate requirement.

But intent doesn't matter in compliance. The *Electricity Safety Act 1998* doesn't have a "we meant to lodge it" defence.
