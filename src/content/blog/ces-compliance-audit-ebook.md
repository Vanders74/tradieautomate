Every Victorian Registered Electrical Contractor works to a COES clock — and it is not one deadline but four. On prescribed work (switchboards, consumer mains, main earthing, solar, battery) the certificate is signed within **4 business days**, Energy Safe is notified within **2 business days** of signing, a Licensed Electrical Inspector inspects within **8 business days**, and copies reach the customer, the distribution business and ESV within **16 calendar days** of completion. The one-month window people quote applies only to the customer copy of a non-prescribed COES. The penalties attach to whichever clock you miss.

But here's what most RECs miss: ESV doesn't just check whether you lodged. They check **what** you lodged, **when** you lodged it, and — in the 2026 audit cycle — **whether your lodgement pattern matches your actual job records**.

A single COES failure might cost you $150–$300 in late fees. A **pattern** of failures — even innocently missed certificates — triggers an audit that can cost $15k–$30k in penalties and months of compliance stress.

## The Gap Nobody Talks About

Most RECs discover they have COES compliance gaps the same way: ESV sends a notice, the REC scrambles to find certificates from 18 months ago, and by then the lodgement window is so far past that the late fee is the least of the problem.

The gap isn't malicious — it's administrative. A certificate forgotten because the job finished on a Friday. A solar install where the admin assumed the CEC paperwork was the COES. An EV charger installation where nobody made the connection between the electrical work and the certificate requirement.

But intent doesn't matter in compliance. The *Electricity Safety Act 1998* doesn't have a "we meant to lodge it" defence.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a COES compliance audit?

A COES compliance audit is Energy Safe Victoria's process of reviewing a REC's Certificate of Electrical Safety lodgement history. ESV cross-checks the REC's job records against COES lodged in the ESV Connect portal to identify missing, late, or incorrect certificates. In 2026 ESV has increased audit activity, particularly in the solar installation sector, and non-compliance can result in infringement notices ($2k–$5k per breach), licence conditions, or suspension.

### How many COES failures trigger an ESV audit?

There's no published threshold — ESV can audit for any number of failures — but in practice RECs with 3+ missing or late COES in a 12-month period are at elevated risk. The 2026 audit cycle has focused on solar and battery installers, where COES compliance gaps are more common. A single audit sweep can uncover multiple COES failures, and penalty exposure can reach $15k–$30k in a single inspection.

### Can a COES late fee be waived?

ESV has limited discretion to waive late lodgement penalties. Genuine administrative errors with no pattern of non-compliance may receive a warning rather than an infringement notice, but repeat late lodgements — particularly where the pattern shows systemic compliance failure — are likely to attract monetary penalties. The safest approach is automated lodgement within 48 hours of job completion.
