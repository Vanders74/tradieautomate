The single biggest financial mistake Australian electricians make isn't technical. It's pricing.

Not the hourly rate that's too low (though that's common). The assumption that **hourly billing is the only way to charge for electrical work**.

The numbers tell a clear story. An electrician billing $110/hour, working 45 chargeable hours a week, 48 weeks a year, grosses $237,600 before costs. Remove overhead, vehicle, insurances, tools, super, and leave — and the take-home is $140k–$160k.

That's not bad. But it's also a ceiling. Every hour you work adds the same $110. Every hour you don't work adds nothing. There is no compound, no leverage, no multiplier.

The alternative isn't "charge more per hour." It's "stop charging by the hour for work that has higher value than the time it takes."

## The Three Pricing Gaps

The e-book focuses on three specific pricing gaps that apply to virtually every electrical contracting business in Australia:

**1. The Compliance Premium**

When a customer is paying $15k for a solar installation, the $39.04 CES lodgement fee isn't the price anchor — the $15k is. The compliance work that wraps around the installation — CES documentation, DNSP notification, design compliance sign-off — is work you already do but don't separately value. The e-book shows how to price compliance-explicit work at 40-60% margin rather than the 20-25% most sparkies achieve on hourly billing.

**2. The Switchboard Upcharge**

Every switchboard upgrade involves a premium for after-hours shutdowns, insurance-required compliance, and the liability of certifying someone else's existing installation. The e-book provides a framework for pricing switchboard work at $3,500–$6,000 (depending on scope) — not the $1,800–$2,500 most electricians quote based on 6 panels and 12 hours labour.

**3. Scope-Creep Recovery**

The biggest margin killer in electrical contracting isn't pricing — it's scope creep. You quote a switchboard replacement, arrive to find asbestos backing, undersized mains, and a customer who "assumed that was included." The e-book includes a variation clause template that recovers $300–$1,200 per job in uncovered scope.
