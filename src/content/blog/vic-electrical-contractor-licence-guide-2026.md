Running an electrical contracting business in Victoria means operating under one of Australia's most actively enforced electrical safety regimes. Energy Safe Victoria (ESV) has been increasing compliance activity across the state — particularly for solar and battery installations — and the Registered Electrical Contractor (REC) framework is the foundation of everything you do legally in the Victorian market.

This guide covers the complete REC registration process for 2026: what it is, who needs it, the REC course requirement, technical and business nominee obligations, insurance, Certificate of Electrical Safety (COES) requirements, and the solar-specific compliance obligations that Victorian electrical businesses need to manage.

---

## Energy Safe Victoria — Victoria's Electrical Safety Regulator

Energy Safe Victoria (ESV) is the independent statutory authority responsible for electrical and gas safety in Victoria. It operates under the *Electricity Safety Act 1998 (Vic)* and the *Gas Safety Act 1997 (Vic)*, and issues licences, registers contractors, sets safety standards, and enforces compliance.

For electrical contractors, ESV is the single agency that handles everything — licensing, registration, Certificate of Electrical Safety (COES) lodgement oversight, and enforcement. Unlike South Australia's dual-body structure, in Victoria your regulatory relationship for electrical contracting sits entirely with ESV.

ESV has also significantly increased its audit activity in recent years, with a particular focus on solar installation compliance — COES lodgement timeliness, system documentation, and AS/NZS 5033 and AS/NZS 5139 adherence. This is not a soft compliance environment.

## The Registered Electrical Contractor (REC) — What It Is

The Registered Electrical Contractor (REC) registration is the licence that authorises a business entity to carry out electrical contracting work in Victoria. Without a current REC registration from ESV, you cannot legally:

- Contract or quote for electrical installation or maintenance work in Victoria
- Issue Certificates of Electrical Safety (COES) for completed electrical work
- Employ or direct licensed electricians to carry out work on your behalf
- Accept payment for electrical contracting work

The REC is a registration, not a personal trade licence. It applies to the business entity — and requires nominated individuals to meet personal qualification requirements on the entity's behalf.

### Three REC Registration Types

| Registration Type | Who It Applies To |
|---|---|
| Sole Proprietor | Individual trading in their own name or under a business name — will be both Technical and Business Nominee |
| Partnership | Two or more people jointly operating a contracting business — one or both partners must be Technical Nominee |
| Corporation | A company (Pty Ltd) structure — requires separate Technical and Business Nominees nominated by the company |

**Family trusts are the exception people miss:** ESV does not register family trust entities at all. If your business trades through a trust, the *trustee company* must be registered as the REC under a company structure. Tell your accountant before you lodge, not after.

## Technical Nominee and Business Nominee Requirements

Every REC registration requires two nominee roles. For a sole proprietor, one person fills both. For partnerships and corporations, these may be separate people.

### Technical Nominee/Supervisor

The Technical Nominee is responsible for the technical supervision of electrical work carried out under the REC. They must:

- Hold a current Victorian **Electrician's Licence (A-Class)** — the personal trade licence issued by ESV
- Meet the qualification requirement with **one** of the following, per ESV's Technical Supervisor Consent Form:
  - Evidence of completing the **Licensed Electrician's Assessment (LEA)** within 5 years of the application (LEA replaced the old Licensed Electrical Mechanics/LEM assessment)
  - Evidence of holding a **'G' class inspector licence**
  - Evidence of completing a **Capstone Assessment** in another state or jurisdiction that ESV recognises as equivalent
  - Evidence of **technical experience** — written references on company letterhead covering roughly 5 years of hands-on installation work

ESV applies a recency rule that catches people out: an LEA completed more than five years before the application date is not accepted on its own. If your assessment is stale, you either provide the experience references or sit the LEA again.

The A-Class electrician's licence is the personal trade licence. Every electrician carrying out installation work in Victoria needs one. If your Technical Nominee's personal licence lapses or is suspended, your REC registration is immediately at risk — ESV requires notification and a replacement supervisor within **10 business days**.

### Business Nominee/Supervisor

The Business Nominee is responsible for the business management and administration of the contracting entity. They must have one of:

- Completed the **Registered Electrical Contractor (REC) Business Course** through an ESV-listed training provider, OR
- Demonstrated relevant business administration and management experience with appropriate qualifications that ESV deems equivalent (e.g. accounting qualifications and relevant management experience)

ESV currently lists six providers of the REC business course: Bendigo Regional Institute of TAFE (Bendigo), NECA Education & Careers (Carlton North), SkillBuild, TAFE Gippsland (Morwell), The Centre for U (North Melbourne) and Victoria University (Sunshine). Course fees vary by provider and run in the high hundreds — budget roughly $900–$1,000 and confirm the current fee directly. The completion certificate must be submitted as part of the REC application.

## Applying for a Victorian REC Registration

REC applications are submitted to Energy Safe Victoria through the ESV licensing portal. The application process involves:

1. **Complete the REC application form** — Available from the ESV website (esv.vic.gov.au) under Electricity Professionals > Licensing and Registration > Registered Electrical Contractors

2. **Business Nominee Consent Form** — Completed and signed by the nominated Business Nominee

3. **Technical Nominee Consent Form** — Completed and signed by the nominated Technical Nominee

4. **LEA or equivalent evidence** — LEA results within 5 years of the application, a 'G' class inspector licence, a recognised interstate Capstone Assessment, or technical experience references

5. **REC Business Course Completion Certificate** — Confirming the Business Nominee has completed the approved course (or equivalent qualification documentation)

6. **Civil liability insurance certificate of currency** — Minimum $5 million cover, in the exact trading name of the entity

7. **Business Name Certificate** — If trading under a business name rather than personal name

8. **Application fee** — **$734.30** for 2026-27 (includes a $70 non-refundable administration component), payable by credit card at lodgement. Renewal is $352.70.

Applications can be submitted in person at ESV's office (Level 5, Building 2, 4 Riverside Quay, Southbank VIC) or via post/electronic submission depending on current ESV processes. Most routine changes — adding or removing a supervisor, updating contact details, renewing — are handled in ESVConnect.

## Civil Liability Insurance — The REC Insurance Requirement

Victorian RECs must hold current civil liability insurance (equivalent to public liability insurance) with **minimum cover of $5 million** against personal injury and/or damage to property caused by the electrical contracting work of the applicant or their employees or agents.

**Key insurance rules for VIC RECs:**

- The certificate of currency must be submitted with your application — tax invoices are not acceptable
- Insurance must be registered in exactly the same entity name as the REC registration (if trading as a business name, the insurance must be in that business name; for partnerships, both partners must be named)
- The policy must remain current throughout the registration period
- If your insurance lapses, your REC registration becomes non-compliant — notify ESV immediately and reinstate coverage

**Insurance exemption — the rule changed:** ESV has lifted insurance exemptions. It no longer requires an application for insurance exemption, and RECs that previously held one without a card are issued a new card automatically on renewal. Running contracting work without the required $5 million cover is a criminal offence, not a paperwork slip — the penalty is **50 penalty units for an individual ($10,455) and 250 penalty units for a body corporate ($52,275)** at the 2026-27 Victorian penalty unit value of $209.10.

## Certificate of Electrical Safety (COES) — Your Post-Installation Obligation

The Certificate of Electrical Safety (COES) is a mandatory compliance document that Victorian RECs must issue for all electrical installation work. It is one of your most critical ongoing obligations — and ESV enforces compliance rigorously.

**When a COES is required:**
- Solar PV system installation (every job — no exceptions)
- EV charger installation (fixed electrical installation)
- Switchboard installation or upgrade
- Any new wiring circuit
- Air conditioning installation
- Any alteration to existing fixed wiring

**Key COES rules and deadlines (prescribed work):**
- The responsible person completes and signs the COES within **4 business days** of completing the work
- Electronic notification to ESV must follow within **2 business days** of completing the certificate — not 2 days from the job itself
- Prescribed work **cannot be energised, or left energised, until the inspector has inspected it** — the inspection comes before connection to supply, not after
- A Licensed Electrical Inspector must inspect within **8 business days** of completion of the work
- The inspector notifies ESV within **4 business days** of the inspection and returns the completed COES to the responsible person within the same 4 business days
- All copies — customer, relevant distribution business and ESV — must be distributed within **4 days** of the inspector returning the completed certificate, and in any event within **16 calendar days of completion of the work**. The responsible person keeps their copy for **3 years**
- For **non-prescribed** work (power points, lighting, air conditioning, hot water), the REC or licensed electrical worker must give the customer a copy of the COES within **30 calendar days**
- Lodged through the **ESVConnect** portal (paper certificates can only be lodged by phone on 1300 360 366)
- Must include sufficient job description to identify the work — generic descriptions like "electrical installation" don't meet ESV's requirements
- The COES is completed and signed by the **licensed electrical worker who did the work**, who is the "responsible person" for it. An employee electrician can sign and lodge a COES in their own name — what they cannot do is contract for the work. Only unlicensed people are barred from purchasing or lodging certificates. If the worker is not the responsible person for the job, they must hand the completed certificate to that person within **4 business days** of finishing

The clock that catches contractors is the 4-then-2 sequence: the certificate itself is signed within 4 business days of the job, and the ESV notification runs from that signature. Leave the certificate until the weekend and the notification deadline moves with it — but the 8-business-day inspection window and the 16-calendar-day distribution deadline do not. Missing any of them is a breach of the *Electricity Safety Act 1998* and exposes you to fines, licence suspension or cancellation.

For a complete guide to COES requirements — including what the document must contain for solar jobs and how to lodge through ESVConnect — see our [Certificate of Electrical Safety Victoria guide](/blog/certificate-electrical-safety-ces-victoria-guide-2026/).

## Maintaining Your REC — the Five-Year Term

Victorian REC registrations are issued for **five years**, and can be renewed for a further period of up to five years. They are not annual. The A-Class electrician's licence is also a five-year licence.

That long cycle is the trap. Five years is long enough that the renewal email lands in an inbox nobody checks any more, and ESV has no discretion about the consequence: it **must cancel** the registration of an electrical contractor who fails to apply for renewal by the renewal date.

**At renewal time:**
- Confirm Technical Nominee still holds a current A-Class electrician's licence
- Confirm Business Nominee details remain accurate
- Provide updated public liability insurance certificate of currency
- Pay the **$352.70** renewal fee (2026-27)
- Notify ESV of any changes to business structure, nominated persons, or trading name

ESV emails a renewal reminder to the business supervisor **3 months before expiry**. That email is the only warning you get — it is the business supervisor's responsibility to keep contact details current, so a stale email address is how RECs get cancelled.

**Critical: Technical Nominee changes**
If your Technical Nominee leaves the business, retires, or their A-Class licence lapses, you must notify ESV **in writing within 10 business days** and nominate a replacement through ESVConnect. The same 10-business-day clock applies to any change in a supervisor's circumstances that affects their ability to supervise, and to any change in the entity's name or legal status. Operating without a current Technical Nominee puts your REC at risk of suspension.

**If your REC is cancelled:** you can apply to reinstate using ESV's REC Reinstatement Form. If the registration has been cancelled for **5 years or more**, the technical supervisor must complete the Licensed Electrician's Assessment again before reinstatement.

**Two obligations most RECs forget** — both are conditions of registration, not optional extras:
- Your **REC number must appear on all advertising**, notices and statements that indicate you carry out electrical contracting work
- You must keep a **register of every licensed electrical worker you employ**, available for inspection by ESV at any reasonable time

Build your REC renewal date — and your Technical Nominee's personal licence renewal date — as separate calendar reminders, flagging 60 days before each expiry. Five-year dates are exactly the ones that get missed.

## Solar-Specific Compliance in Victoria

Victoria has a layered solar compliance framework that Victorian RECs need to manage carefully.

### Clean Energy Council (CEC) Accreditation

Required for design and/or installation of solar PV systems eligible for federal STC rebates under the Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme (SRES). CEC accreditation is separate from your REC — it's issued by the Clean Energy Council and requires annual renewal with Continuing Professional Development (CPD).

Without current CEC accreditation, STCs cannot be assigned for installations your business carries out, removing a key part of the value proposition for residential solar customers.

### Victorian Energy Upgrades (VEU) Program

Victoria's VEU program allows accredited providers to create Victorian Energy Efficiency Certificates (VEECs) for eligible installations — including solar PV. To participate in the VEU program and create VEECs, businesses must be separately accredited by the Essential Services Commission (ESC). This is in addition to (not a replacement for) your REC registration and CEC accreditation.

For solar installers wanting to offer VEU incentives to Victorian commercial customers, check the current activity requirements with the ESC (esc.vic.gov.au) — the C&I solar activity requirements (Activity 47) include specific training and competency obligations beyond standard CEC accreditation.

### AS/NZS Standards Compliance

All solar work in Victoria must comply with:
- **AS/NZS 5033:2021** — Installation and Safety Requirements for PV Arrays
- **AS/NZS 4777** — Grid Connection of Energy Systems via Inverters (DNSP requirements)
- **AS/NZS 5139:2019** — Electrical Installations — Safety of Battery Systems (for battery storage)
- **AS/NZS 3000:2018** — Wiring Rules (for all electrical installation work)

ESV has been citing non-compliance with these standards in enforcement action against Victorian solar installers. Document your compliance with each standard for every solar job — your COES description should reference the relevant inverter model, system capacity, and applicable standards.

### Network Notification (DNSP)

Grid-connected solar systems in Victoria require notification to the relevant Distribution Network Service Provider (DNSP) — AusNet Services, Jemena, CitiPower, Powercor, or United Energy depending on location. The COES lodgement with ESV does not substitute for DNSP notification — both are required. Many Victorian RECs miss the DNSP notification step, particularly on smaller jobs.

## Common Compliance Issues for Victorian Electrical Businesses

**1. COES lodgement delays**
The 4-business-day signing window and the 2-business-day notification that follows it are strictly enforced, and the 16-calendar-day distribution deadline for prescribed work is the real backstop — 30 days applies only to the non-prescribed customer copy. With busy job schedules, it's easy for individual jobs to slip through — particularly at the end of financial year. Build COES creation into your job completion workflow, not as a separate admin task.

**2. Technical Nominee licence lapse**
If the person holding your Technical Nominee role doesn't renew their personal A-Class licence, your REC is at risk. The REC renewal and the personal licence renewal may fall at different times — track them separately.

**3. Insurance name mismatch**
If you trade under a business name and your insurance certificate doesn't exactly match that name, your REC application will be returned. Get this right before submitting — check the exact entity name on all documents.

**4. DNSP notification omissions**
Many Victorian solar installers lodge the COES promptly but forget the DNSP notification. Both are required. Build DNSP notification as a separate step in your solar job completion checklist.

**5. CEC accreditation lapse**
CEC and REC renewals fall at different times and are managed through different portals. They're easy to let drift apart. Set both as calendar reminders — a lapsed CEC accreditation means no STC assignments, which affects every residential solar job you quote.

**6. VEU accreditation confusion**
Some Victorian solar businesses assume their CEC accreditation covers VEU program participation. It doesn't — VEU requires separate ESC accreditation. If you're creating VEECs, confirm your VEU accreditation is current through the ESC.

## Managing Compliance as a Victorian REC

Victoria's layered compliance framework — ESV registration, COES lodgement, CEC accreditation, DNSP notification, VEU accreditation — requires systematic management. The businesses that run into compliance trouble aren't typically ignoring the rules; they're managing the obligations across too many places to track reliably.

The most effective approach is to build compliance milestones into your job management workflow. For every solar job: quote approval → job scheduled → DNSP notification sent → installation complete → COES signed within 4 business days → ESV notified within 2 business days of signing → STC claim submitted. Each step should be a trackable task, not a remembered obligation.

## FAQ

**Can I start an electrical contracting business in Victoria without completing the REC Business Course?**

You need a Business Nominee who has either completed the REC Business Course or who has relevant business administration and management experience with appropriate qualifications that ESV deems suitable (e.g. an accountant with relevant management experience). For most electricians starting a business, completing the REC Business Course is the clearest path — ESV lists six providers, fees run in the high hundreds, and the certificate is accepted by ESV without assessment.

**My Technical Nominee just resigned. What do I do?**

Notify ESV in writing within 10 business days and nominate a replacement through ESVConnect. The replacement must hold a current Victorian A-Class electrician's licence and meet the technical supervisor evidence requirements — LEA completed within the last 5 years, a 'G' class inspector licence, a recognised interstate Capstone, or around 5 years of technical experience references. Do not continue trading without a current Technical Nominee — notify ESV first.

**Do I need a new REC if I change from a sole trader to a company?**

Yes. A REC registration is tied to the specific entity type (sole proprietor, partnership, or corporation). If you restructure from a sole trader to a company, you need to apply for a new REC for the company and cannot transfer the sole proprietor registration. Plan for a transition period where both are current.

**How does Victorian licensing interact with CEC accreditation?**

They're separate obligations managed through different organisations. Your REC registration from ESV authorises you to carry out electrical contracting work in Victoria. CEC accreditation from the Clean Energy Council authorises you to install solar PV systems eligible for STC rebates under the federal scheme. Both are required for residential solar work. The cycles differ — CEC accreditation renews annually with CPD, the REC renews every five years — which is why they drift apart.

**What is the fastest way to have my REC application rejected?**

Submitting incomplete documentation. The most common rejection reasons are: insurance certificate of currency not matching the exact entity name, missing the Business Nominee consent form, LEA certificate not included, or the REC Business Course certificate not submitted. Prepare all documents before submitting — ESV processing is sequential and incomplete applications extend the timeline significantly.
