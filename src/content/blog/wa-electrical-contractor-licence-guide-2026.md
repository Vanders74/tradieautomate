Western Australia operates its own electrical licensing framework, managed by **EnergySafety** — a division of the Department of Energy, Mines, Industry Regulation and Safety (DMIRS). If you're running an electrical contracting or solar installation business in WA, the framework differs materially from the systems in Queensland, NSW, and Victoria — and understanding the differences is the difference between operating legally and running unlicensed.

This guide covers the WA licensing structure for electrical contractors and solar installers in 2026: what licences are required, who needs what, how to apply, and the compliance obligations that catch WA electrical businesses off guard.

---

## The WA Electrical Licensing Framework

Electrical licensing in Western Australia is governed by the *Electricity (Licensing) Regulations 1991* under the *Electricity Act 1945*. EnergySafety administers the scheme and issues all electrical licences.

There are two distinct categories:

**Electrical Worker Licence (personal):** Authorises an individual to carry out specific classes of electrical work. This is the personal licence held by the electrician or solar installer.

**Electrical Contractor's Licence:** Authorises a business entity — individual, partnership, or company — to carry out electrical work for profit: to quote, charge, and employ workers to do electrical work on behalf of customers.

Both are required to legally operate an electrical contracting business in WA.

## Electrical Contractor's Licence

The Electrical Contractor's Licence is issued to the *business entity*, not to an individual. It authorises the business to contract to carry out electrical work.

### Who needs it?

Any person or entity that:
- Agrees (contracts) to carry out electrical installation work for payment
- Employs licensed electrical workers to carry out work on behalf of customers

A solar installation company, an electrical maintenance business, or a sole trader who is paid by customers for electrical work all require a contractor's licence. There is no exemption for small operators.

### Contractor Licence Classes

| Licence Class | Permitted Work |
|---|---|
| Electrical Contractor (Unrestricted) | All classes of electrical work |
| Electrical Contractor (Restricted) | Limited to specific categories only |

Most full-service electrical and solar businesses hold the Unrestricted Contractor's Licence. Solar-only businesses can operate under the unrestricted class without restriction, and it's generally the cleaner choice — you avoid limitations as your service offering expands into EV charger installation, battery storage, or commercial work.

### Requirements for an Electrical Contractor's Licence

1. **Nominated Responsible Supervisor** — The business must nominate at least one individual who holds a valid WA Electrical Worker's Licence (unrestricted, A-grade) as the Responsible Supervisor. The supervisor must be genuinely involved in the supervision of the business's electrical work — not a "paper supervisor" who isn't actually on-site. EnergySafety has explicitly targeted this arrangement in compliance audits.

2. **Insurance** — Public liability insurance at a minimum of $5 million is required. If you employ workers, WorkCover (workers compensation) is mandatory. See our [electrical contractor insurance guide](/blog/electrical-contractor-insurance-australia-2026) for a full breakdown of WA insurance requirements.

3. **Application and fee** — Apply via the EnergySafety online portal. Fees are updated periodically — confirm the current schedule on the DMIRS website before applying.

### Licence Duration and Renewal

WA electrical contractor licences are issued for periods of up to 3 years. With a 3-year term, it's easy to lose track of expiry dates. Set a calendar reminder 90 days before expiry. Renewal requires current insurance details and a current Responsible Supervisor nomination. A lapsed licence means you cannot lawfully contract for electrical work — even for a single day — until it's renewed.

## Electrical Worker's Licence (Personal)

The Electrical Worker's Licence authorises an individual to carry out electrical work. WA issues several classes:

| Licence Class | Description |
|---|---|
| Unrestricted (A-Grade) | Full scope of electrical installation and maintenance work |
| Restricted | Limited to specific work categories |
| Apprentice | In-training only, under direct supervision |

In WA, electricians working in solar and battery storage use their standard A-grade electrical worker licence — there is no separate solar-specific personal licence at the state level. CEC Accreditation (federal, issued by the Clean Energy Council) is the solar-specific credential that sits alongside the state licence.

## Solar PV Licensing in WA

Solar PV installation sits at the intersection of WA state licensing and federal CEC accreditation:

**WA state requirements:**
- Electrical Contractor's Licence (unrestricted)
- Responsible Supervisor holds valid WA Electrical Worker's Licence
- Compliance with AS/NZS 5033 (solar PV installation) and AS/NZS 5139 (battery storage)

**Federal requirements:**
- CEC Accreditation — required for STC-eligible installations under the SRES. For a full breakdown of what CEC auditors check, see our [CER audit prep guide](/blog/cer-audit-prep-solar-installers).

Both are required. The CEC accreditation doesn't replace the WA state licence. A common mistake for interstate solar businesses expanding into WA is assuming their CEC accreditation is sufficient — it's not.

### Network Connection in WA

Western Power (the distribution network for most of WA) has its own technical requirements for grid-connected solar systems. Key rules in 2026:

- Systems over 5kW (single phase) or 30kW (three phase) require a formal network application
- Export limiting may be required in some network areas — the [CSIP-Aus export limit rules](/blog/csip-aus-export-limit-1-5kw-solar) are relevant for WA solar installations
- Switchboard upgrades may be required depending on existing metering infrastructure

Check Western Power's *Embedded Generator Connection Requirements* (EGCR) for current specifications before commissioning any grid-tied system.

## Certificate of Compliance for Electrical Installations (CCEI)

In WA, all new electrical installation work — including solar PV and battery storage — must be certified by a Certificate of Compliance for Electrical Installation (CCEI). This is WA's equivalent of the CES in Victoria or the CCEW in NSW.

The CCEI must be:
- Issued by the licensed electrical contractor after completion of the installation
- Submitted to Western Power (or the relevant supply authority) before connection
- Retained by the contractor as a compliance record

Failure to issue a CCEI is a serious compliance breach. Unlike some other compliance documents, there's no informal grace period in WA — the CCEI must be in place before power is connected to a new installation.

Build CCEI generation into your job completion workflow as a non-optional step. If you're not already using a [digital job management system for solar installers](/blog/digital-job-management-solar-installers), a paper-based CCEI process at scale creates real compliance exposure. A post-installation checklist that triggers CCEI issuance as part of the job sign-off process eliminates the risk.

## Record Keeping Requirements

Contractors must maintain records of all electrical work carried out — including CCEIs, job documentation, and compliance records — for a minimum of 7 years. These records can be requested by EnergySafety during a compliance audit at any time within that window.

Most WA electrical businesses underinvest in record-keeping until they receive an audit request. The [hidden admin cost calculator](/blog/hidden-admin-cost-calculator) can help quantify the cost of managing records manually versus a system that captures everything automatically against each job.

## Employing Electricians in WA

If you employ electricians in your WA electrical business:

**Workers compensation:** Mandatory for all employees in WA. Failure to hold workers compensation insurance is an offence with significant penalties.

**Supervision requirements:** All electrical work must be performed by, or under the direct supervision of, a licensed electrical worker. Unlicensed persons cannot carry out electrical work unsupervised. For solar businesses running multiple install teams simultaneously, define your supervision model clearly in writing.

**Apprentice supervision:** Apprentices must work under direct supervision of a qualified tradesperson at all times. Check apprenticeship authority requirements and supervision ratios — they vary by year of apprenticeship.

## Common Compliance Mistakes for WA Electrical & Solar Businesses

**1. Operating without a contractor's licence**
Completing electrical work without a valid contractor's licence is an offence under WA law. Penalties are significant, and unlicensed work creates liability exposure for both the contractor and the customer.

**2. Responsible Supervisor genuineness**
EnergySafety specifically audits for supervisors who are nominated on paper but not genuinely involved in the business's work. If your nominated Responsible Supervisor isn't regularly on-site or available for supervision, you're at risk.

**3. Not issuing CCEIs**
Forgetting or delaying CCEI issuance is the most common compliance failure for WA solar businesses. There's no workaround — get it built into your completion workflow.

**4. Letting the contractor's licence lapse**
With 3-year licence terms, expiry dates slip. Lapse for a day and you cannot legally contract for work. Set your 90-day reminder now.

**5. Not checking Western Power export rules**
Installing a 10kW system in a network area capped at 5kW export without notifying Western Power creates a compliance issue that can result in the customer being disconnected and the installer facing enforcement action.
