Australia's solar incentive landscape is a patchwork of federal schemes, state programs, and network rebates — and for solar installers, navigating it efficiently is both a competitive advantage and an administrative burden. Businesses that handle rebate paperwork smoothly close more jobs; businesses that make it complicated lose customers to competitors who simplify the process.

This guide covers every major solar incentive available in 2026 by state, what installers need to know, and how to manage the paperwork efficiently.

## Federal: Small-scale Technology Certificates (STCs)

The STC scheme is the backbone of Australian solar affordability. Every CEC-accredited solar installation generates a number of STCs based on:

- **System size** (kW of installed capacity)
- **Location zone** (zones 1–4 — higher irradiance = more STCs)
- **Years remaining in the scheme** (STC multiplier decreases each year until 2030)

### How STCs work in practice for installers

1. Customer accepts quote (which includes a reduction for STC value)
2. Customer signs STC assignment form, transferring their STCs to the installer or a registered STC agent
3. Installer completes the installation with CEC-approved components
4. Installer (or agent) lodges STCs with the Clean Energy Regulator
5. Agent purchases STCs from installer at current market rate

The STC "rebate" is typically presented to customers as an upfront discount rather than a separate government payment. STC value fluctuates with the spot market — typically $35–$40/STC, though this varies.

### Current STC values by system size (approximate, 2026)

| System size | Zone 3 (Sydney/Melbourne) | Zone 4 (Brisbane/Perth) |
|---|---|---|
| 6.6 kW | ~$2,500–$3,000 | ~$3,000–$3,500 |
| 10 kW | ~$3,800–$4,500 | ~$4,500–$5,200 |
| 13.2 kW | ~$5,000–$5,800 | ~$6,000–$7,000 |

*Approximate values based on 2026 STC market. Actual value depends on current spot price and exact zone.*

### CEC accreditation requirement

Only CEC-accredited installers can generate STCs. If you're not yet CEC-accredited, the Clean Energy Council accreditation process is the prerequisite for accessing any federal solar incentive. Accreditation requires completing the relevant CEC training and design assessment.

## Victoria — Solar Homes Program

### Battery storage rebate

- **Value:** Up to $8,800 rebate
- **Eligibility:** Victorian households earning under $210,000 combined annual income; property value under $3 million; existing solar or installing solar at the same time
- **How installers participate:** Must be registered under the Solar Victoria Code of Conduct. Registration is annual and requires CEC accreditation, valid insurance, and compliance with Solar Victoria's design and installation standards.
- **Application:** Installer applies on behalf of customer through the Solar Victoria portal after installation

### Solar panel rebate

The Victorian solar panel rebate has been reduced and availability is limited in 2026 — Solar Victoria operates the program in rounds. Check solar.vic.gov.au for current round status and remaining allocation.

### Installer registration tip

Solar Victoria's CES (Code of Conduct) registration requires maintenance — annual renewal, installation record submission, and compliance with any updated standards. Keep your registration current to avoid delays when a customer qualifies.

## South Australia — Home Battery Scheme

- **Value:** Interest-free loan up to $3,000 (not a grant — repaid over time)
- **Eligibility:** SA households installing a qualifying battery storage system; income testing may apply
- **Installer requirement:** Must be an authorised Home Battery Scheme installer (separate registration from CEC accreditation)
- **Application:** Installer submits application through the SA government portal on behalf of the customer

### For installers

The SA scheme is loan-based, meaning customers benefit from deferred costs rather than a direct government payment. When quoting, clearly explain this distinction — "interest-free loan" is different from a rebate and affects the customer's upfront cash requirement.

Keep your authorised installer registration current — applications submitted by non-registered installers are rejected and delay the job.

## Queensland

Queensland doesn't have a state-funded solar or battery rebate equivalent to Victoria's program. Solar incentives available in QLD:

**Ergon and Energex feed-in tariffs** — Network solar feed-in tariffs are set by the Queensland Competition Authority annually. These are ongoing payments for excess solar exported, not upfront rebates.

**Queensland Battery Booster** — A previous state battery storage rebate program. Check the QLD government website for current status — programs change between budget cycles.

**Local government programs** — Some Queensland councils offer additional incentives for solar or battery installation. Brisbane City Council and others have run periodic programs.

For Queensland installers, the STC scheme remains the primary incentive.

---

## New South Wales

NSW doesn't have a state-funded solar panel rebate in 2026. Key incentives:

**Ausgrid and Endeavour network solar bonuses** — Some distribution network service providers offer additional feed-in payments or connection incentives in specific circumstances. Check with the relevant DNSP for current programs.

**NSW Empowering Homes Program** — An interest-free loan scheme for solar-battery systems up to $14,000 for eligible households. Check NSW government website for current availability and eligibility.

**CCEW compliance note** — NSW requires a Certificate of Compliance for Electrical Work for every solar installation. All solar electrical work must be done by a licensed electrical contractor, and the CCEW must be lodged within 7 days of completion.

---

## Western Australia

**Synergy FIT (Feed-in Tariff)** — WA's Synergy offers a feed-in tariff for Synergy customers with eligible solar systems. Rate varies — check Synergy's current published rate.

**Distributed Energy Buyback Scheme (DEBS)** — WA's DEBS pays for solar and battery export. Battery systems that export during peak periods receive a higher rate.

**WA battery incentive programs** — WA has piloted virtual power plant (VPP) programs that include incentives for battery storage. Check Synergy and Horizon Power for current offerings.

---

## Australian Capital Territory

**Sustainable Household Scheme** — The ACT's Sustainable Household Scheme offers zero-interest loans for eligible home upgrades including solar, battery storage, and EV chargers. Loan amounts up to $15,000, repaid through rates notices. CEC-accredited installers are required.

## How to Manage Rebate Paperwork Efficiently

The biggest administrative challenge for solar installers handling multiple state rebate programs simultaneously is keeping the compliance documentation, customer eligibility checks, and application submissions organised across jobs.

**The most effective setup:**

1. **STC assignment in the quote workflow.** Use your job management software to include the STC assignment form at the quote stage — customer signs at quote acceptance, not separately post-installation.

2. **State rebate eligibility checklist.** Build a pre-job checklist in your job management system that covers the eligibility criteria for your state's rebate programs. Complete this at the site survey — before the install, not after.

3. **Installation documentation linked to job.** Photos, compliance documents, and equipment serial numbers are all attached to the specific job record. When the state rebate application asks for this information, it's all in one place.

4. **Automated post-installation follow-up.** Many customers ask about rebate status weeks after installation. An automated follow-up sequence (via your job management system or CRM) that updates customers at key milestones reduces inbound enquiries.
