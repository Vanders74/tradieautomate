If you're comparing the two, the choice often comes down to where your business sits on the complexity spectrum and what you're willing to pay.

### AroFlo

AroFlo publishes one rate and one setup figure, and nothing else: **$80 per user per month with a minimum of three users**, so a sole trader pays for three licences ($240/month) whether they use them or not. The per-user rate slides down as you add licences, but AroFlo does not publish the tiers, so you cannot calculate your own bill above three users without a quote.

Two things to pin down before you compare totals:

- **GST treatment is not stated on AroFlo's pricing page.** If $80 is ex-GST, the three-user floor is $264/month including GST, not $240. Ask for the figure in writing.
- **The setup fee is the published number.** AroFlo's own FAQ puts the one-off Smart Start cost at **$1,450 AUD, or $2,900 with implementation** — data imports, accounting integration and a training program. It is not part of the subscription, and it is non-refundable if you churn in month three. AroFlo is currently advertising **50% off setup** as a spring promotion, dated October 2026, which halves that number for as long as the promotion runs.

AroFlo has been part of the Simpro Group since 2021, which is why its functionality increasingly overlaps simPRO's at the top end.

### Why other comparison sites quote $39, $65 or $67 for AroFlo

If you have three tabs open and the numbers do not match, this is why. As at October 2026, the directory listings and older reviews still carry pre-increase figures: GetApp and Capterra both show **$39/user**, AroFlo's own blog still quotes **$67/user with a $335 monthly minimum**, and two comparison blogs list a **$195–$235 three-user minimum**. The practical effect is that our $240 floor looks high next to a $117 floor. It isn't — those figures are older plans that are no longer sold. Use the published $80 × 3, then add the setup fee, because the directories do not include it at all.

## Compliance Documentation

Both platforms have compliance documentation capabilities, but they approach it differently.

### ServiceM8

ServiceM8's compliance tooling is purpose-built for the way Australian licensed trades actually work — with two limits worth understanding before you buy it expecting a certificate printer:

**Custom form builder.** Any compliance form — COES templates for electricians, plumbing compliance certificates, SWMS for high-risk work, ARCtick refrigerant records — can be built as a digital form. Field staff complete it on-site, on their phone, and the result is generated as a PDF attached to the job. Building forms requires the **Growing plan ($79/mo)**; forms bought from the Form Store are a one-off purchase and work on any plan, including Free. There is no pre-built CCEW or COES form shipped by ServiceM8.

**What it does not do: lodge the certificate.** ServiceM8 does not submit anything to a regulator in any state. In NSW the CCEW goes through Service NSW eCert; in Victoria the COES goes to ESV, with prescribed work requiring the responsible person to notify ESV within 2 business days of completing the certificate and an inspection within 8 business days of the work. ServiceM8 captures the evidence (photos, test results, signatures, timestamps) and gives you the PDF. The lodgement is still your job, and no field service platform on the market does it for you.

**Photos: timestamped and attached, not GPS-stamped.** Photos taken through the ServiceM8 app attach to the job record with a timestamp, visible in the job diary alongside the job's address and check-in data. The image file itself does not carry GPS coordinates — coordinate stamping comes from a third-party add-on (Photo GPS), which works on the iOS app and online dashboard but not the Android Lite app. If your documentation standard requires coordinates in the file, plan for the add-on and know the platform gap.

**Enforced completion.** Forms can be set as mandatory and fields marked required, so the job cannot be closed out with the form half-done. This removes the most common compliance failure — paperwork that doesn't get done because no one was chasing it.

This approach works well for the compliance-heavy trades that make up the majority of ServiceM8's user base — electricians, solar installers, plumbers, gas fitters, HVAC technicians.

### AroFlo

AroFlo has comprehensive compliance and safety management tools, including more sophisticated permit-to-work management, safety inspection scheduling, and integrated asset maintenance compliance. For larger operations managing complex compliance frameworks — preventive maintenance programs across large asset portfolios, contractor compliance management, enterprise safety management — AroFlo's compliance features are more powerful.

For a 5-person electrical business managing COES documentation and solar installation compliance, AroFlo's compliance depth is significantly more than required — and the setup to configure it correctly adds weeks to your implementation.

## Job Costing and Financial Reporting

### AroFlo's Advantage

AroFlo has more sophisticated financial reporting than ServiceM8. Detailed job costing with multiple cost centres, project budgeting, progress billing, comprehensive profitability reports by client, job type, division, and period — AroFlo's financial reporting is built for businesses that need to manage complex job economics.

For trade businesses with multiple business units, complex project revenue recognition, or significant commercial project work, AroFlo's financial reporting provides visibility that ServiceM8 doesn't match.

### ServiceM8's Approach

ServiceM8 provides functional job costing — time tracking, materials logging, and job cost summary — alongside Xero/MYOB integration that handles detailed accounting. For most small to mid-size trade businesses, this combination is sufficient. Detailed financial analysis lives in Xero or MYOB; ServiceM8 feeds it.

For businesses with very sophisticated financial management requirements at the job level, AroFlo's reporting may justify the cost premium.

## Mobile Experience

### ServiceM8

The ServiceM8 app is designed around a fast job card: open the job, take photos, complete the form, invoice, take payment, close out — in a few taps. It works offline and syncs when the phone reconnects, which matters on sites with no signal.

Both platforms now ship a native app on iOS and Android, so this is no longer a platform-parity argument. The difference is depth versus speed: AroFlo's app exposes more of the platform (job costing lines, asset records, timesheets), ServiceM8's exposes less and loads faster. If your technicians only need to complete jobs and capture evidence, the lighter app is the easier one to get adopted. If you want field labour and materials landing straight into job costing as they happen, that needs the heavier one.

### AroFlo

AroFlo has a mobile app that covers the core field functionality — job access, time logging, materials capture, photo upload. It's functional and works. The user experience reflects the complexity of the underlying platform — there's more to navigate.

For field technicians who need fast, simple job completion, ServiceM8's mobile experience is consistently the more field-friendly option.
