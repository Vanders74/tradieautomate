Fergus is a New Zealand-founded job management platform with a growing Australian user base. It sits in the mid-tier of the market — more capable than Tradify on job costing, simpler and cheaper than simPRO, but with a distinct set of trade-offs that make it the right choice for some businesses and the wrong choice for others.

This review gives you an honest view of what Fergus does well, where it falls short, and who it actually suits in 2026.

---

## What Makes Fergus Different

Fergus was built with job costing at its centre. The platform tracks actual vs estimated labour and materials on every job, making it easier to understand which work is profitable and which isn't. This is genuinely useful for businesses where margin visibility matters — particularly in HVAC, plumbing, and gas work where material costs vary significantly.

The other distinguishing feature is its supplier integration. Fergus integrates with trade suppliers (including some Australian distributors) to pull live pricing into quotes and purchase orders, reducing manual price updates.

## What Fergus Does Well

### Job costing and margin visibility

The standout capability. Fergus tracks estimated vs actual cost at the job level — labour hours, materials used, and margin percentage. For a business that quotes fixed-price jobs, this visibility into where margin is being lost is valuable.

The job profitability summary is accessible without running reports — the dashboard shows margin status per job in real time. Businesses regularly discovering 10–20% margin variance between quotes and actuals find this clarity useful.

### Quote management

Fergus's quoting is clean and allows detailed line-item pricing with labour and material separation. Suppliers with integrated pricing can auto-populate material costs. Converting approved quotes to jobs is one-click.

### Supplier integration

Fergus has integrations with trade suppliers for live pricing on materials. This is stronger than ServiceM8's product catalog (which is manually maintained) and useful for businesses doing high-volume material purchasing.

### Clean interface

Fergus's interface is clean and relatively straightforward. The learning curve is manageable for non-technical business owners. Setup is faster than simPRO or AroFlo, though slower than ServiceM8 or Tradify.

### Xero and MYOB integration

Both are well-supported — invoices, contacts, and payments sync. The integration quality is comparable to ServiceM8.

---

## Where Fergus Falls Short

### No CCEW compliance form

Like Tradify, Fergus has no pre-built CCEW form for NSW electricians. Document attachment is available, but there's no structured digital form completion workflow. For electrical businesses where CCEW completion is a daily or weekly requirement, this is a significant limitation.

### Per-user cost that undercuts its value proposition

At 3–5 users, Fergus costs more than ServiceM8 while offering fewer compliance-specific tools. This is the fundamental challenge with Fergus's value proposition for electrical businesses — the job costing benefit has to outweigh both the extra cost and the missing CCEW workflow.

### Less mobile polish than ServiceM8

ServiceM8's mobile app is genuinely best-in-class in the Australian market. Fergus's mobile app is functional but less refined. The "On My Way" automated customer notification, GPS tracking with customer ETA link, and on-screen CCEW completion are all ServiceM8 features without a Fergus equivalent.

### Smaller Australian user community

Fergus has stronger NZ roots and NZ user adoption. In Australia, ServiceM8 and Tradify have larger communities, more Australian-specific feature development, and more local resources. This affects the quality of Australian-specific advice available online.

## The Verdict

Fergus is a solid mid-tier platform with genuine strengths in job costing and supplier integration. For plumbing, gasfitting, and HVAC businesses where margin visibility is a priority and CCEW compliance isn't a daily requirement, it's worth a serious look.

For Australian electricians specifically, the missing CCEW form is a real problem. And at $49/user/month, Fergus is often more expensive than ServiceM8 for comparable or smaller teams — while offering less on the compliance documentation front.

If you're choosing between Fergus and ServiceM8 as an electrician:
- If CCEW is a regular part of your work → ServiceM8
- If you're NSW-based doing regular installation work → ServiceM8
- If job costing visibility is your primary unmet need and you're a plumber/gasfitter → Fergus deserves a trial

At minimum, use both free trials and test the actual workflow for your business type before committing.
