## Where Does the Time Actually Go?

Most tradies think their admin problem is "a bit of paperwork." The reality, when you break it down task by task, is closer to a part-time job.

Here's what the research shows — and what we consistently hear from trade business owners across Australia.

### Quoting: 2–5 Hours Per Week

Writing up a quote sounds simple. In practice, it involves:

- **Site visit or phone consultation** to understand the scope
- **Measuring, costing materials, estimating labour** — often done manually or on spreadsheets
- **Formatting and sending the quote** via email, sometimes handwritten
- **Follow-up calls** a few days later because the customer hasn't responded
- **Revisions** when the scope changes, or the customer wants a cheaper option

For a solar installer quoting a $15,000 system, this can easily run to 3–4 hours per quote. At a conservative $100/hour effective rate, that's $300–$400 before you've won the job.

Miss 30% of the quotes you send (which is average), and that wasted quoting time adds up to thousands of dollars per month in lost productivity.

### Invoicing and Payment Collection: 2–4 Hours Per Week

Invoicing sounds like five minutes. But here's the full picture for a typical trade business doing 10–15 jobs per month:

- Creating and sending invoices (often delayed because you're still on-site at 5pm)
- Following up on unpaid invoices at 30, 60, and 90 days
- Reconciling payments against jobs in your accounting software
- Handling disputes, requests for variations, or "I thought that was included" conversations

**The cash flow cost is often worse than the time cost.** A study of Australian small businesses found that the average SME has $38,000 in outstanding receivables at any given time. For trade businesses, late payment is endemic — and every day an invoice sits unpaid is a day you're effectively lending that money to your customer interest-free.

### Scheduling and Dispatch: 1–3 Hours Per Week

If you're running even two or three technicians, scheduling without a proper system is a constant battle:

- **Phone tag with customers** to confirm job timing
- **Re-scheduling when jobs run over** and the next customer needs to be notified
- **Coordinating parts and materials** arrivals with job start times
- **Communicating last-minute changes** to your team

The hidden cost here isn't just your time — it's the customer experience. A missed confirmation call or a late arrival because nobody updated the calendar costs you Google reviews and repeat business.

### Customer Follow-Up: 1–2 Hours Per Week

This is the category that surprises most trade business owners. Think about:

- **Post-quote follow-up** — "Did you get my quote? Do you have questions?"
- **Post-job check-ins** — making sure the customer is happy before they go online and complain
- **Review requests** — asking satisfied customers to leave a Google review (which most businesses do manually, if at all)
- **Maintenance reminders** — for trades with recurring work like HVAC servicing or solar panel cleaning
- **Warranty and compliance document delivery** — especially critical for solar and electrical work

Each of these interactions has value. But done manually, they're also a constant drain on your time and attention.

## Why Tradies Underestimate Their Admin Overhead

When we ask trade business owners to estimate their weekly admin time, the average guess is around 4–5 hours. When we actually track it task by task, the real number is almost always 8–12 hours.

Why the gap?

**Admin time is invisible.** You don't clock off and think "I just spent 2 hours on scheduling." You think "I had a busy day." The hour of email in the morning, the 20-minute quote call that turned into a 45-minute back-and-forth, the invoices you did at 9pm on Thursday — they don't feel like a coherent block. They feel like part of running a business.

**You've never benchmarked it.** If you've always spent 10 hours a week on admin, that's your normal. You have no reference point for what it would feel like to spend 4 hours on admin instead.

**The opportunity cost is invisible too.** When you spend Tuesday afternoon chasing invoices, you're not acutely aware of the two service calls you could have done instead. The lost revenue doesn't appear on any report. It just doesn't happen.

The calculator above is designed to make this visible. Even if your estimate is conservative, the number at the bottom is usually enough to shift the frame.
