An unresolved electrical defect notice can suspend your contractor licence and compound across every job tied to that notice. The problem is that most guidance on this topic is a government PDF — written for auditors, not contractors.

This guide translates the regulatory framework into plain language: what a defect notice actually is, the state-by-state response process, and how to avoid getting one in the first place.

---

## What Is an Electrical Defect Notice?

An electrical defect notice (also called a Compliance Notice, Rectification Notice, or Improvement Notice depending on the jurisdiction) is a formal legal document issued by an authorised person directing a licensed electrical contractor to rectify a specified non-compliance in completed electrical work.

Notices are issued by:

- **State electrical safety regulators** — Energy Safe Victoria (ESV), SafeWork NSW, Electrical Safety Office Queensland (ESO), EnergySafety WA, Consumer and Business Services SA, WorkSafe ACT, NT WorkSafe, WorkSafe Tasmania
- **Network distributors** — Ausgrid, Endeavour Energy, Essential Energy, CitiPower, Powercor, Energex, Ergon, Western Power, SA Power Networks, TasNetworks, Evoenergy
- **Clean Energy Regulator (CER)** — for solar PV and battery installations under the *Renewable Energy (Electricity) Act 2000*

### Notice Classifications

| Classification | Description | Typical Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| **Minor** | Administrative or documentation failures — missing test records, incorrect labelling, incomplete CCEW | 30–60 days |
| **Major** | Installation non-compliance — wrong RCD type, cable sizing, earthing deficiency, enclosure IP rating | 7–30 days |
| **Immediate Danger** | Safety-critical defect posing immediate risk of shock, fire, or electrocution | 24 hours or same-day isolation |

An immediate-danger notice may include a direction to de-energise the installation until the defect is rectified. That means the property owner is without power — which creates significant commercial and relationship pressure on the contractor.

## State-by-State: Who Issues, What Applies, How to Respond

### Victoria — Energy Safe Victoria (ESV)

**Regulator:** Energy Safe Victoria  
**Legislation:** *Electricity Safety Act 1998 (Vic)*  
**Notice type:** Compliance Notice, Improvement Notice, Prohibition Notice  
**Response deadline:** Specified on notice — typically 7–28 days for major defects, immediate for prohibition notices  
**Appeal pathway:** Victorian Civil and Administrative Tribunal (VCAT) — file before deadline for automatic stay consideration

ESV issues Certificates of Electrical Safety (CES) compliance as a condition of contractor licensing. Non-compliance with a Compliance Notice is grounds for suspension or cancellation of an Electrical Contractor Licence under s.46 of the *Electricity Safety Act 1998*.

ESV Technical Bulletin TB-080 covers RCD requirements and is a common basis for defect notices following AS/NZS 3000:2018 Amendment 2.

**ESV contact:** esv.vic.gov.au | 1800 800 158

---

### New South Wales — SafeWork NSW

**Regulator:** SafeWork NSW (installation safety); NSW Fair Trading (licensing and CCEW)  
**Legislation:** *Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (NSW)*, *Electricity (Consumer Safety) Act 2004 (NSW)*  
**Notice type:** Improvement Notice, Prohibition Notice  
**Response deadline:** Specified on notice — Improvement Notices set a reasonable rectification period; Prohibition Notices are immediate  
**Appeal pathway:** Internal review → NSW Civil and Administrative Tribunal (NCAT)

Key distinction: SafeWork NSW handles the on-site electrical safety aspects; NSW Fair Trading handles CCEW compliance and contractor licence matters. A defect with documentation failures (e.g., missing CCEW, late lodgement) will involve Fair Trading. An installation defect may involve SafeWork NSW.

Under the *Home Building Act 1989 (NSW)*, contractors also have statutory warranty obligations — a defect notice can trigger both regulatory and civil liability.

**SafeWork NSW contact:** safework.nsw.gov.au | 13 10 50  
**NSW Fair Trading:** fairtrading.nsw.gov.au | 13 32 20

---

### Queensland — Electrical Safety Office (ESO)

**Regulator:** Electrical Safety Office (ESO), part of Office of Industrial Relations  
**Legislation:** *Electrical Safety Act 2002 (Qld)*  
**Notice type:** Electrical Safety Improvement Notice, Electrical Safety Prohibition Notice  
**Response deadline:** Specified on notice — typically 7–21 days for improvement notices  
**Appeal pathway:** Review under *Electrical Safety Act 2002* Part 11 — application to Queensland Industrial Relations Commission (QIRC)

Queensland uses a risk-based inspection regime with mandatory self-reporting for serious electrical incidents under s.40 of the *Electrical Safety Act 2002*. Failure to report is a separate offence.

**ESO contact:** worksafe.qld.gov.au/laws/electrical-safety | 1300 362 128

---

### Western Australia — EnergySafety

**Regulator:** EnergySafety, part of Department of Energy, Mines, Industry Regulation and Safety (DEMIRS)  
**Legislation:** *Electricity (Licensing) Regulations 1991 (WA)*  
**Notice type:** Defect Notice, Order  
**Response deadline:** Typically 5–14 days for defect notices; orders may be immediate  
**Appeal pathway:** State Administrative Tribunal (SAT)

WA uses a direct Defect Notice framework under the *Electricity (Licensing) Regulations*. Defect notices in WA are directly tied to the licencing regime — accumulating notices without resolution will trigger a licensing review.

**EnergySafety contact:** demirs.wa.gov.au/energysafety | 1800 025 620

---

### South Australia — Consumer and Business Services (CBS)

**Regulator:** Consumer and Business Services (CBS), Office of the Technical Regulator (OTR)  
**Legislation:** *Electricity Act 1996 (SA)*  
**Notice type:** Notice to Rectify, Notice of Non-Compliance  
**Response deadline:** Typically 10–28 days  
**Appeal pathway:** SACAT (South Australian Civil and Administrative Tribunal)

SA's Office of the Technical Regulator handles electrical installation compliance. CBS handles licensing. Solar installation defects may also involve the SA Home Battery Scheme compliance team under SA Power Networks referrals.

**CBS/OTR contact:** cbs.sa.gov.au | 131 882

---

### ACT — Access Canberra / WorkSafe ACT

**Regulator:** Access Canberra (licensing); WorkSafe ACT (installation safety)  
**Legislation:** *Electricity Safety Act 1971 (ACT)*  
**Notice type:** Improvement Notice, Prohibition Notice  
**Response deadline:** Specified on notice  
**Appeal pathway:** ACT Civil and Administrative Tribunal (ACAT)

**Contact:** accesscanberra.act.gov.au | 13 22 81

---

### Tasmania — WorkSafe Tasmania

**Regulator:** WorkSafe Tasmania  
**Legislation:** *Electrical Safety Act 1998 (Tas)*  
**Notice type:** Improvement Notice, Prohibition Notice  
**Response deadline:** Specified on notice  
**Appeal pathway:** Magistrates Court

**Contact:** worksafe.tas.gov.au | 1300 366 322

---

### Northern Territory — NT WorkSafe

**Regulator:** NT WorkSafe  
**Legislation:** *Work Health and Safety (National Uniform Legislation) Act 2011 (NT)*  
**Notice type:** Improvement Notice, Prohibition Notice  
**Response deadline:** Specified on notice  
**Appeal pathway:** NT Civil and Administrative Tribunal (NTCAT)

**Contact:** worksafe.nt.gov.au | 1800 019 115

## Common Triggers: What Gets You a Defect Notice

### Solar Installations

Solar PV work generates a disproportionate share of defect notices nationally. The most common triggers:

| Trigger | Standard | Regulator Most Likely to Catch It |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong RCD type (Type AC when Type A required) | AS/NZS 3000:2018 Amd 2, CEC Design Guidelines | ESV, CER, DNSPs |
| Missing DC isolator labelling | AS/NZS 5033:2021 cl. 5.4 | CER, DNSPs |
| Incorrect inverter settings (export limit, voltage) | DNSP technical requirements | DNSPs on commissioning |
| Missing or incorrect arc fault protection | AS/NZS 5033:2021 | CER audits |
| Cable sizing — DC strings undersized for voltage drop | AS/NZS 5033:2021 | CER audits |
| Inadequate battery installation labelling | AS/NZS 5139:2019 | ESV, CER |
| STC claim lodged with incomplete documentation | Renewable Energy (Electricity) Act 2000 | CER |

### Switchboard Work

- **RCD non-compliance** — after AS/NZS 3000:2018 Amendment 2 (effective January 2024), RCD requirements changed for circuits supplying socket outlets and lighting circuits in new installations and major alterations. Switchboard upgrades without updated RCD provision are a common trigger.
- **Insufficient surge protection** — required under the amended wiring rules for new switchboard installations in certain configurations.
- **Neutral conductor not identified** — recurring documentation/labelling failure in switchboard work.

### General Electrical Work

- **Undersized cable** — particularly on long cable runs where voltage drop wasn't calculated
- **Inadequate earthing** — bonding of metallic services, earth electrode resistance
- **Enclosure IP rating** — outdoor or wet area installations where the enclosure IP rating doesn't meet AS/NZS 3000 requirements

### Documentation Failures

A "documentation defect" is still a defect. Missing CCEWs, late CES lodgements, and incomplete test records are issued as notices just as readily as installation defects in some jurisdictions.

## Step-by-Step Response Process

### Step 1 — Receive and Read the Notice Carefully

Note: the issuing authority, the specific defect described, the notice classification, the rectification deadline, and the appeal process. Photograph the notice. Log it in your job management system against the relevant job record.

### Step 2 — Assess the Classification

- **Immediate danger:** Contact the property owner immediately. Arrange isolation or attendance same day. Do not leave the defect unaddressed overnight.
- **Major defect:** Assess whether the deadline is achievable. If not, contact the issuing authority before the deadline to request an extension — document the contact in writing.
- **Minor/documentation:** Begin rectification immediately. Don't treat a longer deadline as permission to delay.

### Step 3 — Fix the Defect

Carry out the required rectification work to the standard required by the notice. If the defect notice references a specific standard (e.g., AS/NZS 3000:2018), ensure the fix meets that standard — not just the symptom described.

### Step 4 — Document the Fix

This is where many contractors underdo the paperwork and set themselves up for a follow-up notice.

- Photograph the before and after state (timestamped, geo-tagged if possible)
- Record test results for any testing required as part of the fix
- If a CCEW, CES, or other compliance document is required for the rectification work itself, issue it

### Step 5 — Lodge the Response

Most jurisdictions require written notification that the defect has been rectified:

- **ESV (VIC):** Lodge via MyESV portal — upload photos and documentation
- **SafeWork NSW:** Notify the inspector named on the notice in writing, with supporting evidence
- **ESO (QLD):** Submit via the Electrical Compliance Reporting System (ECRS)
- **EnergySafety (WA):** Notify the issuing inspector directly with documentation
- **CER:** Respond via the Clean Energy Regulator's online portal

Keep a copy of everything you submit and request confirmation of receipt.

### Step 6 — Follow-Up Inspection

Many major defect resolutions require a follow-up inspection by the issuing authority before the notice is formally closed. Confirm with the inspector whether a re-inspection is required and schedule it proactively rather than waiting to be contacted.

## How to Avoid Defect Notices

### Pre-Inspection Checklist — Solar Installations

Before completing any solar job:

- [ ] RCD type correct for solar string inverter (Type A minimum, Type B for three-phase and some battery inverters — check ESV TB-080 and CEC guidelines)
- [ ] All labelling present: DC isolators, AC isolators, switchboard markings, array location diagram
- [ ] Inverter settings verified against DNSP technical requirements (export limit, voltage, VAR response mode)
- [ ] All DC cable sizing calculated for voltage drop ≤ 3% under AS/NZS 5033
- [ ] Battery system installed to AS/NZS 5139 — clearances, ventilation, labelling
- [ ] CEC installation report data complete before leaving site
- [ ] CCEW (or state equivalent) issued within required timeframe

### Pre-Inspection Checklist — Switchboard Work

- [ ] RCD coverage meets AS/NZS 3000:2018 Amd 2 requirements for the scope of work
- [ ] All circuits labelled correctly
- [ ] Earth continuity and insulation resistance test results recorded
- [ ] Surge protection device installed where required
- [ ] IP rating of enclosure verified for installation environment

### Documentation Hygiene That Prevents Audit Findings

- Capture test results digitally on-site, not on paper that lives in the ute
- Attach geo-tagged photos to the job record before leaving site
- Issue compliance certificates (CCEW, CES) the same day as job completion, not at end of week
- Never estimate or transpose test results — if you can't read a figure, retest it

## What Happens If You Ignore a Defect Notice

Ignoring a defect notice is one of the fastest ways to lose your electrical contractor licence.

| Jurisdiction | Consequence of Non-Response |
|---|---|
| VIC (ESV) | Licence suspension or cancellation under *Electricity Safety Act 1998* s.46 |
| NSW (SafeWork / Fair Trading) | Prohibition on further work; licence referral to Fair Trading; WHS Act prosecution |
| QLD (ESO) | Electrical Safety Act 2002 prosecution; licence suspension |
| WA (EnergySafety) | Licence review; prosecution under *Electricity (Licensing) Regulations 1991* |
| All states | CER may suspend your STC authorisation — blocking solar installation work nationally |

The consequence is not just regulatory. Property owners and insurers can pursue civil claims for defective work, and an unresolved defect notice on a property is a material fact in any insurance or property sale proceeding.
