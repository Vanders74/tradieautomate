## What Is a CCEW?

A Certificate of Compliance Electrical Work (CCEW) is the certificate required by the *Gas and Electricity (Consumer Safety) Act 2017 (NSW)* and the *Gas and Electricity (Consumer Safety) Regulation 2018 (NSW)*. It certifies that a safety and compliance test was carried out on the electrical installation work in accordance with the Australian/New Zealand Wiring Rules (AS/NZS 3000) and the other requirements of the Regulation.

**As of 1 July 2026, all CCEWs must be lodged digitally through the mandatory BCNSW eCert portal** — paper forms and old submission methods are no longer accepted. The one exception arriving later is eligible CER work (solar, battery, inverters up to 200 kW), which moves to the NSW CER Installer Portal from November 2026 — covered below. See our [full CCEW compliance guide](/blog/ccew-nsw-electrical-compliance-guide-2026/) for the complete lodgement workflow and portal registration steps.

The certificate is lodged by the **tester** — the qualified person who conducted the safety and compliance test. That is the individual contractor licence or qualified supervisor certificate holder who did or supervised the work; a company contractor licence cannot even be registered in eCert.

---

## What Triggers the CCEW Requirement? When Is a CCEW Legally Required?

A CCEW is required for any electrical installation work that involves installing, repairing, adding or altering electrical equipment on an installation (see our [full CCEW compliance guide](/blog/ccew-nsw-electrical-compliance-guide-2026/) for the complete regulatory framework). The frequent real-world triggers are:

1. **A change to the main switchboard** — adding circuits, replacing the switchboard, upgrading protection devices (MCBs, RCDs), or modifying the main switch
2. **An increase in connected load** — any addition of new circuits, high-load appliances, EV chargers, air conditioning units, or solar inverters that increase the total connected demand on the supply
3. **New electrical installations** — any new wiring installed in new or existing buildings, including extensions, renovations, or outbuildings
4. **Solar PV system installation or upgrade** — solar installations that connect to the grid or increase system capacity
5. **Battery storage systems** — installation of AS/NZS 5139-compliant battery storage
6. **Replacement work** — like-for-like swap-outs of water heaters, ovens, cooktops and electrical accessories

**The like-for-like trap:** most guides still list replacement of fittings and appliances as exempt. Building Commission NSW says the opposite — advisory note 2/2026 names water heaters, ovens, cooktops and electrical accessories as work that requires a CCEW. If your business has been treating "same unit, same wiring" as paperwork-free, that is the most likely place you are exposed.

## The CCEW 7-Day Rule (2026): When the Clock Starts

The CCEW 7-day rule is where most compliance failures happen.

Under s37A of the Gas and Electricity (Consumer Safety) Regulation 2018, the tester must give the certificate to the Secretary **no later than 7 days after completing the safety and compliance test** — by lodging it on BCNSW eCert, or by giving the relevant information to an eligible entity within the same 7 days.

"Completed" means the test is finished and the installation has been verified as safe to energise. Not when the customer pays. Not when the paperwork is done. Not when it's convenient.

**Seven days is a backstop, not a grace period.** Building Commission NSW's own electrical compliance page states the certificate must be submitted "as soon as practical, but no later than seven days after the electrical test has been completed." Advisory note 2/2026 puts it the same way: no later than 7 days after completing the work, including the safety and compliance test. So a job tested Monday morning and lodged the following Monday is inside the maximum but outside the standard — the obligation is prompt lodgement, and the 7-day figure only caps it.

Seven days is not "this week." A test completed on a Wednesday has a lodgement deadline of the following Wednesday. A job tested on a Thursday afternoon before a long weekend is already tight.

### Who Receives the CCEW?

The certificate is **lodged with the Secretary (Building Commission NSW)** — that is the recipient s37A names, and eCert is the only channel. The tester can satisfy the duty either by lodging on eCert or by giving the relevant information to an **eligible entity** (a business approved to lodge on the tester's behalf) within the same 7 days; under s37B that entity then has 7 days from receipt to lodge.

Copies to other parties are a distribution function of the lodgement, not a separate statutory deadline:

1. **The customer (property owner)** — emailed automatically if you enter their email when lodging. There is no standalone 7-day duty to hand the customer a copy; the obligation is lodgement, and eCert does the distribution.
2. **The relevant network distributor** — where required, for example new installations or work affecting the network connection (Ausgrid, Endeavour Energy, Essential Energy)
3. **Your employer or principal contractor** — if their email is included

Note: the network distributor may have separate notification requirements (work notification for metering changes or new connections) with different timeframes. The CCEW is a separate document to the connection application.

## The Fines for Non-Compliance (2026 Penalties)

The penalties under NSW law for CCEW failures are significant and apply per offence — not per licence.

**s37A lodgement offence:** maximum 20 penalty units for an individual (about $2,200 at the NSW penalty unit value of $110) and 40 penalty units otherwise (about $4,400)
**On-the-spot fines:** up to $1,000 for each occasion a CCEW is not supplied to Building Commission NSW
**Technical standards breach:** up to $550,000 for failure to carry out electrical installation work in accordance with the technical standards in the Regulation
**Repeated non-compliance:** licence conditions or suspension, with the record now attached to the individual's eCert history

Watch the number you'll see everywhere else: **$22,000 for an individual and $110,000 for a corporation is the Home Building Act 1989 penalty scale for unlicensed contracting**, not the CCEW lodgement penalty. It gets copied from guide to guide and has nothing to do with certificates of compliance.

Building Commission NSW's Electrical Compliance Team investigates complaints, and electrical work disputes between homeowners and contractors frequently trigger certificate audits. If the CCEW wasn't lodged, wasn't lodged on time, or doesn't accurately reflect the work performed, the tester carries the liability.

The risk is asymmetric. The fine for non-compliance far exceeds the administrative cost of getting the documentation right.

## How Much Does a CCEW Cost?

There is **no government lodgement fee** for a CCEW — it is submitted free through the BCNSW eCert portal. The cost most electricians associate with CCEWs is the time spent preparing and submitting the form, which is why many bill it as a separate line item (commonly **$50–$95 plus GST**) rather than absorbing it into the job.

The real cost is the penalty for not lodging one: **20 penalty units for an individual (about $2,200) and 40 otherwise (about $4,400)**, plus on-the-spot fines up to $1,000 per missed certificate — see our [full CCEW compliance guide](/blog/ccew-nsw-electrical-compliance-guide-2026/) for the complete penalty schedule and lodgement workflow.

## Where Businesses Fall Short

The pattern we see most commonly in electrical contracting businesses:

**The Friday afternoon backlog.** Jobs are completed throughout the week. The CCEW paperwork sits in a pile on the desk or in someone's email inbox. Friday afternoon becomes a compliance panic — trying to reconstruct which jobs need what documentation, what the testing results were, and whether the 7-day window has already closed for anything from earlier in the week.

**Technician vs. administrator disconnect.** The licensed contractor (who signs the CCEW) isn't always the person who did the work or captured the testing data. When the testing results are in a paper form in the technician's ute and the contractor is trying to sign off in the office, critical data gets missed or estimated.

**Solar-specific complexity.** For solar installations, the CCEW requirements interact with the inverter registration, the STCs claim process, and the distributor connection approval. The paperwork chain is longer and the consequences of gaps are higher.

**Lost test results.** The most common failure mode isn't contractors who don't know the rules — it's test results that were captured on paper, then lost, damaged, or illegible. If you can't produce the test results, you can't produce a compliant CCEW. Digital capture at the point of test eliminates this entirely.

## CCEW vs Other NSW Electrical Compliance Documents

It's worth distinguishing the CCEW from other documents in the NSW electrical compliance stack:

**CCEW (Certificate of Compliance Electrical Work):** Lodged by the individual tester (not the company licence holder). Certifies the work complies with applicable standards. Given to the Secretary via BCNSW eCert within 7 days; copies then flow to the customer and, where relevant, the network distributor.

**Electrical Installation Safety Inspection Certificate:** Required where an installation has been de-energised for 6 months or more and is being re-energised. A qualified electrician carries out the safety inspection and completes the certificate for the customer, who gives it to their electricity retailer — it is not lodged with Building Commission NSW. Separate document, separate trigger to the CCEW. (Note there is no NSW "Electrical Safety Certificate" — that naming belongs to Victoria's Certificate of Electrical Safety.)

**Connection/Service Alteration Notice:** Required for work affecting the metered supply point or network connection. Issued to the network distributor separately from the CCEW.

**Design registration:** Some larger or non-standard installations require the electrical installation design to be verified and documented before work commences — check with Building Commission NSW and your network distributor for the thresholds that apply to your job. This is separate from the CCEW issued on completion.

Understanding which documents are required for which job types is fundamental compliance knowledge for any NSW electrical contractor. The CCEW is required for the broadest category of work — but it isn't the only document in the compliance picture.

## The Solar-Specific CCEW Workflow

Solar installations deserve specific attention because the paperwork chain is longer and more complex:

**Standard solar installation triggers:**
- New switchboard circuits for inverter connection → CCEW required
- Increased connected load → CCEW required
- Any metering changes (smart meter installation by distributor) → connection notification required

**The solar documentation chain for NSW:**
1. Grid connection application to network distributor (before installation)
2. CCEW returned within 7 days of the test — **but for eligible CER work from November 2026 this happens inside the NSW CER Installer Portal, not eCert** (see the next section; copies then auto-distributed to the customer and, where required, the distributor)
3. CEC installation report / STC paperwork lodged with the Clean Energy Regulator
4. Any required metering change notification to the distributor

Missing any step in this chain creates both regulatory and commercial problems — STC claims can't be lodged without the installation certificate, and the distributor won't approve export if the connection notification hasn't been received.

For the complete solar compliance documentation workflow including CER requirements and audit preparation, see our [solar compliance checklist](/blog/solar-compliance-checklist-2026/).

### From November 2026: solar and battery leave eCert for the CER Installer Portal

This is the one change that overrides the 7-day eCert workflow above for a large share of CCEW work, and it lands within weeks.

The **NSW Consumer Energy Resources (CER) Installer Portal** becomes mandatory for new and upgrading CER systems up to **200 kW inverter capacity** — rooftop solar, battery storage and their inverters — where the DNSP connection application is submitted and approved from the start date:

| Network | Start date |
|---|---|
| Endeavour Energy and Essential Energy | 2 November 2026 |
| Ausgrid | 23 November 2026 |

For those jobs the portal handles the CCEW *and* the AEMO Distributed Energy Resources Register record in one commissioning submission. The NSW Government's position is explicit: for eligible CER work the portal replaces the existing obligation to complete the CCEW through BCNSW eCert and to submit a separate DERR record. **You do not lodge a second CCEW in eCert for the CER work itself.**

Three things that catch people out:

1. **The 7-day clock does not disappear, it moves.** The CCEW is generated as part of commissioning, so the practical discipline is the same — capture test results at commissioning, not at month end. If the installation includes electrical work outside the CER scope (a sub-board for a shed, extra GPOs, switchboard upgrades unrelated to the inverter), a **separate CCEW still goes through BCNSW eCert for that work**, on the same 7-day rule.
2. **The trigger is the connection application date, not the install date.** A job whose DNSP application was approved in October is outside the mandate; one approved on 2 November is inside it, whichever network it sits on.
3. **The EBM requirement rolls out by network**, managed by each DNSP — so a 200 kW commercial install in one network and an identical one in another can have different go-live obligations in the same week.

For work completed before your network's start date, the eCert workflow on this page applies in full. We keep the [full CCEW compliance guide](/blog/ccew-nsw-electrical-compliance-guide-2026/) updated with the portal detail as each DNSP publishes its arrangements.

## BCNSW eCert Portal — NSW Electrical Compliance & What Changed on 1 July 2026

As of 1 July 2026, all CCEWs must be submitted through the **BCNSW eCert portal**. This was previously the "NSW Fair Trading portal" — the system has been rebranded and centralised under Building Commission NSW, and the PDF form, Essential Energy portal, and paper NECA book submission methods are no longer accepted — **mandatory digital lodgement** through BCNSW eCert is the only valid pathway.

**If you're still submitting CCEWs the old way, you're now non-compliant.** Every CCEW submitted through the old channels after 1 July 2026 is treated as not submitted. The s37A lodgement offence is 20 penalty units for an individual (about $2,200) and 40 penalty units otherwise (about $4,400), plus on-the-spot fines of up to $1,000 for each occasion a CCEW is not supplied. These sit separately from the $550,000 maximum for failing to work to the technical standards in the Regulation — a different offence.

**What to do now:**
- Register at [ecert.buildingcommission.nsw.gov.au](https://ecert.buildingcommission.nsw.gov.au/) (free — you register with your individual electrical licence or qualified supervisor certificate, matching your name exactly, plus 2FA. Company and partnership contractor licences are not accepted for registration; the individual licence or QS holder lodges)
- BCNSW eCert auto-distributes copies to the customer, the DNSP, and Building Commission NSW
- See our [complete CCEW NSW compliance guide](/blog/ccew-nsw-electrical-compliance-guide-2026/) for the full step-by-step lodgement workflow, solar-specific requirements, and common audit triggers

The compliance gap is a data-capture problem. If testing data is captured digitally on-site, at the time of work, the CCEW can be generated and issued the same day — without a Friday afternoon scramble.

## Building a Systematic CCEW Process (2026)

The businesses that never have compliance failures aren't necessarily the ones with the most careful people — they're the ones with the best systems. Here's what a systematic CCEW process looks like:

**Step 1 — Trigger identification.** Every time a job is created, the system prompts "Does this job require a CCEW?" The answer is captured before the job is dispatched.

**Step 2 — On-site capture.** The job diary template includes all required testing fields. The technician cannot mark the job complete without completing the testing checklist.

**Step 3 — Same-day generation.** The office generates the CCEW from the digital job data on the day the job is closed — not at end of week.

**Step 4 — Multi-party distribution.** The CCEW is emailed to the customer, submitted to the relevant distributor portal, and lodged with Building Commission NSW — all on the same day.

**Step 5 — Record archiving.** The CCEW and all supporting test documentation are attached to the job record in your job management system. Retrievable in seconds, kept to your own retention policy (seven years is the common contractor standard).

This is not a complicated system. It's a disciplined process. The discipline is built into the software workflow so it doesn't rely on individual memory.

## Summary: The CCEW Checklist

When a job is complete, ask these questions before your technician leaves site:

- [ ] Does this job involve a switchboard modification? → CCEW required
- [ ] Does this job add new circuits or increase connected load? → CCEW required
- [ ] Does this involve new wiring, solar, or battery storage? → CCEW required
- [ ] Has all required testing been completed and recorded? → Must be captured before sign-off
- [ ] Has the CCEW been lodged on BCNSW eCert? → Required within 7 days of the test
- [ ] Is the customer's email on the certificate? → That is how eCert sends them their copy
- [ ] Has the network distributor been notified (if applicable)? → Check network requirements

If the answer to any of the first three is yes and the rest of the checklist isn't done, your business has an open compliance liability.

Build the checklist into your job completion workflow. The 7-day clock starts on completion day — not paperwork day.

## FAQ

### Does a CCEW need to be submitted for every electrical job in NSW?

No — but the test is much broader than most electricians assume. Building Commission NSW's advisory note 2/2026 (issued August 2026, updated September 2026) requires a CCEW for **any** electrical work that installs, repairs, adds or alters electrical equipment on an installation, and it expressly names like-for-like replacement of water heaters, ovens, stove tops and electrical accessories. So a straight swap of an oven or a cooktop is certificate work in NSW. What falls outside is work that does not touch the installation at all — testing and tagging a portable appliance, for example. When uncertain, issue one: the cost of an unnecessary certificate is trivial next to the penalty for a missing one.

### Who can sign a CCEW in NSW?

Lodgement is the tester's job. Under s37A of the Gas and Electricity (Consumer Safety) Regulation 2018, the obligation sits on the qualified person who conducted the safety and compliance test — an individual contractor licence holder, or a qualified supervisor certificate holder, who did or supervised the work. A company contractor licence cannot be registered in BCNSW eCert, so the individual who tested the installation lodges the certificate even where a company holds the contracting licence. That individual's signature is the declaration that the work was tested against AS/NZS 3000.

### What happens if the 7-day CCEW deadline passes?

Missing the 7-day deadline is a compliance breach and exposes the contractor to the applicable penalties (20 penalty units for an individual, about $2,200, and 40 penalty units otherwise under s37A, plus on-the-spot fines up to $1,000). If you've missed the deadline, issue the CCEW as soon as possible — a late CCEW is better than none — but be aware that you may still be subject to penalties for the delay. Contact your industry association for advice on your specific situation.

### Can a CCEW be issued electronically?

It is not optional. Since 1 July 2026 the CCEW must be lodged through BCNSW eCert (individually or by approved API) — handwritten and PDF forms are no longer accepted, so a paper certificate is not a compliant substitute. Customers receive their copy by email when their address is entered on the form.

### Does solar battery storage require a CCEW in NSW?

Yes — the CCEW is still required, but from November 2026 the **lodgement route** changes. Battery storage and rooftop solar up to 200 kW inverter capacity are CER systems, and from **2 November 2026** (Endeavour Energy and Essential Energy) or **23 November 2026** (Ausgrid), new and upgrading CER systems whose DNSP connection application is submitted and approved from that date must be commissioned through the [NSW CER Installer Portal](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/nsw-plans-and-progress/regulation-and-policy/installer-portal).

The portal completes the CCEW and the AEMO DERR record in the one submission — you do **not** lodge a separate CCEW in BCNSW eCert for that CER work. Any non-CER electrical work on the same job (extra circuits, an unrelated sub-board) still needs its own CCEW through eCert, inside the same 7 days.
