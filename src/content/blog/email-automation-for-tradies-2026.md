A 5-person electrical crew that automates quote follow-ups recovers roughly $15,000 per year in forgotten leads — money already earned through marketing and reputation, left on the table because no one sent a second email.

**Email automation for tradies** is the practice of using software to send pre-written, timed email sequences — such as quote follow-ups, review requests, and service reminders — triggered automatically by customer actions like enquiry submission or job completion, without manual intervention.

Most trade businesses lose 30–40% of their leads by never following up after a first quote. The fix isn't hiring an admin — it's setting up four automated workflows that run while you're on the tools.

---

## The Play

Stop losing leads because no one followed up. Set up four automated email sequences — quote follow-up, post-job review request, annual service reminder, and invoice overdue nudge — using a free or low-cost email platform. Spend two hours building them once, then let them recover revenue every week while you focus on billable work.

The play works because trade businesses don't have a sales team. When a lead goes quiet after a quote lands, there's no one whose job it is to check in. Automation fills that gap at near-zero marginal cost.

## Exact Execution Steps

### Workflow 1: Quote Follow-Up Sequence

This is the highest-ROI automation for any trade business. When a customer requests a quote — whether through your website, a phone call, or a platform like ServiceM8 or [hipages](https://hipages.com.au) — add their email to a three-email sequence.

1. **Set up a list in your email platform** called "Quote Follow-Up." Brevo's free tier covers this with unlimited contacts.
2. **Create a sign-up trigger** — if you use a website form, connect it directly. If you're collecting emails manually, batch-import them weekly.
3. **Build the three-email sequence** with the timings below. All three emails run automatically once a contact enters the list.
4. **Remove contacts who reply or book** — set a rule to exit the sequence when someone responds to any email.

#### Template: Quote Follow-Up #1 — Immediate Thank-You

**Subject:** Your [Trade] Quote from [Business Name] — Here's What's Next

Hi [First Name],

Thanks for the opportunity to quote your [job type] at [address/suburb].

I've attached your detailed quote. Here's what happens from here:

- The quote is valid for 30 days
- We can usually schedule within [X] weeks of acceptance
- If you have questions about any line item, reply to this email or call me on [phone]

If you're ready to go ahead, just reply "yes" and I'll lock in a date.

Cheers,
[Your Name]
[Business Name]
[Phone]
[Licence Number]

#### Template: Quote Follow-Up #2 — 48-Hour Check-In

**Subject:** Quick question about your [Trade] quote

Hi [First Name],

Just checking you received the [job type] quote I sent on [day]. Happy to walk through anything that doesn't make sense — I know [trade] pricing isn't always obvious from a document.

No pressure either way. If you've decided to hold off, that's completely fine — just let me know so I can update my schedule.

[Your Name]

#### Template: Quote Follow-Up #3 — 7-Day Final Nudge

**Subject:** Last call — [Job Type] quote for [Suburb]

Hi [First Name],

My [job type] quote for [suburb] expires in two weeks, and I'm locking in the schedule for next month this Friday. If you're still interested, now's the best time to book — I've got [X] slots available and they typically fill by mid-month.

If you've gone another direction, no worries at all. Appreciate the chance to quote.

[Your Name]
[Phone]

**Expected result:** A 3-email quote follow-up sequence typically converts 10–15% of leads that would otherwise go cold — for a business sending 100 quotes/month at an average job value of $2,500, that's $30,000–$45,000 in additional annual revenue from leads you already generated.

### Workflow 2: Post-Job Review Request

Google reviews are the single biggest trust signal for trade businesses. Most customers are willing to leave a review — they just need a prompt at the right moment.

1. **Trigger:** Job marked complete in your job management system.
2. **Delay:** Send 2-3 days after completion, while the job is still fresh but the customer has had time to assess the work.
3. **Single email, no sequence:** Review requests don't need multiple follow-ups — one well-timed email is enough.

#### Template: Post-Job Review Request

**Subject:** How did we do with your [job type]?

Hi [First Name],

Thanks for trusting us with your [job type] last [day of week]. I hope everything's working as expected.

If you've got 30 seconds, a quick Google review makes a huge difference for a small business like ours. Here's the direct link:

[Insert Google Review Link]

And if anything's not right, please call me directly on [phone] — I'd rather fix it than find out through a review.

Thanks again,
[Your Name]

**Expected result:** Consistent review requests lift review volume by 30–50% within 3 months. For an electrical contractor doing 20 jobs/week, that's 8–10 new reviews per month versus 2–3 without prompting.

### Workflow 3: Annual Service Reminder

Electrical safety inspections, solar panel cleaning, air conditioner servicing, hot water system checks — most trades have recurring work they're not capturing because no one reminds the customer.

1. **Set up a segment** for past customers whose job date is 11+ months ago.
2. **Automate a single reminder** email at the 12-month mark with a link to book.
3. **Repeat annually** — remove from the list once they rebook, re-add 11 months later.

#### Template: Annual Service Reminder — Electrical Safety Check

**Subject:** 12 months since your last electrical check — time for a service?

Hi [First Name],

It's been 12 months since we completed your [job type] at [suburb]. Under AS/NZS 3000, regular electrical safety checks are recommended to catch issues before they become hazards — and with winter/summer [seasonally relevant], now's a good time.

A standard safety inspection takes about 60 minutes and costs $[price range]. If you'd like to book, just reply to this email or call [phone] — I've got [X] slots available this month.

[Your Name]
[Licence Number]

**Expected result:** Even a 10% rebooking rate on a customer base of 500 past clients generates 50 additional jobs per year. At an average ticket of $350 for a safety inspection, that's $17,500 in new revenue from work that was already yours to capture.

### Workflow 4: Invoice Overdue Nudge

Chasing late payments is the admin task tradies hate most — and the one they procrastinate on, extending your average payment cycle.

1. **Trigger:** Invoice unpaid at 7 days past due date.
2. **Sequence:** Two emails — a polite nudge at 7 days, a firmer reminder at 14 days.
3. **Fallback:** If unpaid at 21 days, the system alerts you to make a phone call.

#### Template: Invoice Reminder #1 — 7 Days Overdue

**Subject:** Quick reminder — Invoice #[Number] for [Job Type]

Hi [First Name],

Just a friendly reminder that invoice #[number] for the [job type] completed on [date] is now 7 days past due. The outstanding amount is $[amount].

If payment has already been made, please ignore this email and thank you. Otherwise, you can pay online here:

[Payment Link]

Any questions, give me a call on [phone].

[Your Name]

#### Template: Invoice Reminder #2 — 14 Days Overdue

**Subject:** Invoice #[Number] — 14 days overdue, please arrange payment

Hi [First Name],

Invoice #[number] for $[amount] is now two weeks past due. I need to ask you to arrange payment by [date — 7 days from now] to avoid any further action.

If you're having difficulty, please call me on [phone] — I'd rather work something out than escalate.

Pay online: [Payment Link]

[Your Name]

**Expected result:** Automated invoice follow-ups typically reduce average payment time from 28–35 days to 14–18 days. For a business with $50,000 in outstanding invoices at any given time, halving the payment cycle frees up $25,000 in working capital.

## Spam Act 2003: What Australian Tradies Need to Know

Email automation for trade businesses is legal in Australia, but you must comply with the **Spam Act 2003**, administered by the **Australian Communications and Media Authority (ACMA)**. The rules are straightforward, and most trade businesses comply naturally if they follow basic practices.

**The three requirements:**

1. **Consent** — The recipient must have agreed to receive your emails. Consent can be **express** (they ticked a box on your website form) or **inferred** (they're an existing customer, or they contacted you about a quote — this creates an existing business relationship). Sending cold emails to people you've never interacted with is a violation.

2. **Identify** — Every email must clearly identify you as the sender. Your business name, ABN, and contact details should be in the footer. No anonymous or misleading sender names.

3. **Unsubscribe** — Every automated email must include a functioning unsubscribe link. Brevo, Mailchimp, and all reputable platforms include this automatically. If someone unsubscribes, remove them from all sequences within 5 business days.

**Penalties:** The ACMA can issue infringement notices of up to $2.5 million for serious or repeated breaches. However, enforcement is almost entirely directed at bulk spam operations, not trade businesses sending legitimate follow-ups to their own customers. If your recipients are people who've already done business with you or asked for a quote, you're operating within the law.

**Practical compliance checklist for tradies:**

- Add an unsubscribe link to every email (Brevo/Mailchimp does this automatically)
- Include your business name, ABN, and phone number in the footer
- Only email people who have enquired, booked, or opted in
- Honour unsubscribe requests immediately
- Keep records of how each contact entered your list (a note in your CRM or email platform is sufficient)

## Common Failure Modes

- **Sending too many emails too fast.** A 5-email sequence over 5 days feels aggressive for trade customers. Space follow-ups at least 48 hours apart. If someone doesn't respond after three touches, move them to a quarterly newsletter list rather than continuing to chase.

- **Using generic templates that sound like marketing.** Trade customers respond to emails that sound like they came from a real electrician or plumber, not a corporate marketing department. Use the templates above as a starting point and add your own voice. Write like you talk on site.

- **Forgetting to remove converted leads from sequences.** Nothing erodes trust faster than sending "just checking if you're still interested" to someone who booked last week. Set up automation rules that remove contacts from follow-up sequences when they reply or when their status changes in your job management system.

- **Neglecting list hygiene.** If 20% of your emails bounce, email providers start flagging you as spam. Clean your list quarterly: remove invalid addresses, unsubscribe non-responders after 6 months of no engagement, and only add contacts who have a genuine connection to your business.

- **Not tracking which emails perform.** Brevo and Mailchimp both show open rates and click rates. If your 48-hour follow-up has a 12% open rate while your 7-day follow-up has 35%, adjust the timing. You won't know unless you look.

## FAQ

### How much does email automation cost for a trade business?

Brevo's free tier covers 300 emails/day and basic automation — sufficient for most small trade businesses. Paid plans start at approximately $15/month when you need more volume or advanced features. Mailchimp's automation features require the Essentials plan from ~$20/month. Total cost for a fully automated email setup ranges from $0–$25/month for most trade businesses.

### Can I automate SMS follow-ups instead of email?

Yes — Brevo and Mailchimp both support SMS automation on paid tiers. However, SMS costs per message (typically $0.05–$0.12 per SMS in Australia) versus email's near-zero marginal cost. SMS works well for job reminders (higher open rate, more urgency), but email is more cost-effective for quote follow-ups and service reminders. ServiceM8's native SMS reminders handle job confirmations without additional cost.

### What if a customer asks me to stop emailing them?

Remove them from all automated sequences immediately. Under the Spam Act 2003, you must honour unsubscribe requests within 5 business days. Brevo and Mailchimp handle this automatically through their unsubscribe links — the contact is suppressed across all lists. If a customer asks verbally, manually unsubscribe them in your email platform.

### Do I need a privacy policy for email automation?

Yes. Australian businesses collecting personal information (names, email addresses, phone numbers) must have a privacy policy under the Privacy Act 1988. Your privacy policy should state what data you collect, how you use it, and that you won't share it with third parties. Most trade business websites link to a privacy policy in the footer — if you don't have one, the [OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au) provides templates for small businesses.

### How long before I see results from email automation?

Quote follow-up sequences typically show results within 2–4 weeks — the first time a previously-cold lead books from a follow-up email, the system has paid for itself. Review requests take 1–3 months to build momentum. Annual service reminders pay off at the 12-month mark when the first batch triggers. Invoice nudges show immediate cash flow improvement from the first cycle.
