If you're running an electrical contracting business in Queensland — whether that's residential solar installs, commercial fit-outs, or general electrical maintenance — you need the right licence. In Queensland, that means navigating a framework managed by two separate agencies: Electrical Safety Queensland and the Queensland Building and Construction Commission (QBCC). Add in QLeave obligations and CEC accreditation for solar, and most new contractors are juggling four or five different compliance requirements simultaneously.

This guide covers what's required in 2026: licence categories, who needs what, how to apply, ongoing obligations, and the common compliance traps that cost Queensland electrical businesses real money.

---

## Who Regulates Electrical Contractors in Queensland?

Electrical licensing in Queensland sits across two agencies, and understanding which one covers what is the first thing to get right:

**Electrical Safety Queensland (Office of Industrial Relations):** Issues electrical worker licences — the personal licence that allows an individual to carry out electrical work — and the electrical contractor licence, which authorises a business to *contract* to perform electrical work for customers.

**Queensland Building and Construction Commission (QBCC):** Issues the contractor's licence required for any building work valued over $3,300 in Queensland. Most electrical installation work — including solar — qualifies as "building work" under the Queensland Building and Construction Act, so a QBCC licence is required alongside the ESQ contractor licence.

For solar and electrical installation businesses, you typically need both. Many contractors come unstuck because they hold one and assume it covers everything.

## Electrical Contractor Licence (Electrical Safety Queensland)

The electrical contractor licence is issued under the *Electrical Safety Act 2002* and authorises a business entity — individual, partnership, or company — to carry out electrical work for profit.

### Who needs it?

You need an electrical contractor licence if you:
- Carry out electrical installation, maintenance, or repair work for customers
- Employ or engage electricians to do electrical work on your behalf
- Operate a solar installation business where electrical connection work is involved

A sole trader who is also a licensed electrical worker can hold the contractor licence themselves. Companies must nominate a licensed electrical worker as their "responsible person."

### Contractor Licence Classes

| Licence Class | Scope |
|---|---|
| Electrical Contractor (Unrestricted) | Full scope of electrical installation work |
| Electrical Contractor (Restricted) | Limited to specific work categories only |

Most full-service solar and electrical businesses will operate under the unrestricted class. Solar PV installation involves grid connection work that requires an unrestricted or appropriately endorsed licence.

### Application Requirements

To apply for an electrical contractor licence:

1. **Hold a valid electrical worker licence** — You (or your nominated responsible person) must hold a current Queensland electrical worker licence (A-grade or relevant restricted licence).
2. **Meet the financial requirements** — ESQ assesses whether you have adequate financial resources to operate.
3. **Public liability insurance** — Minimum $5 million public liability cover required.
4. **Complete the application** — Via the Electrical Safety Queensland online portal.

## QBCC Contractor Licence

The QBCC contractor licence is required for building work valued over $3,300 (including labour and materials). Solar PV installation is classified as building work in Queensland, making QBCC licensing mandatory for any solar contracting business.

### Licence Categories Relevant to Electrical & Solar

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| Electrical work (low voltage) | Standard residential and commercial electrical installation |
| Solar (photovoltaic) | Solar PV installation, including battery storage |

A solar electrical business will typically hold both categories, or the electrical low voltage licence with solar as a permitted trade.

### Financial Requirements (QBCC MFR)

The QBCC applies a **Minimum Financial Requirements (MFR)** framework. Your required financial resources scale with your annual turnover:

| Annual Revenue | Net Tangible Assets Required |
|---|---|
| Up to $200,000 | $12,000 |
| $200,001–$400,000 | $24,000 |
| $400,001–$800,000 | $46,000 |
| Over $800,000 | Calculated individually |

You must submit annual financial reporting to the QBCC — either a self-declaration (for lower-turnover businesses) or a formal accountant's report. Missing your MFR filing is one of the most common reasons Queensland contractors have their licences suspended — it happens automatically, with no grace period.

For businesses where the accountant handles MFR reporting, sharing a simple financial snapshot from your accounting software (Xero, MYOB) each year makes the process significantly faster. See our [best accounting software for solar and electrical businesses](/blog/best-accounting-software-solar-electrical-australia-2026) guide for platforms that integrate with QBCC reporting workflows.

### Applying for a QBCC Licence

1. Determine your licence category and turnover band
2. Gather financial statements (last completed financial year)
3. Nominate your Qualified Supervisor (must hold relevant technical qualifications)
4. Submit application via the QBCC portal with the applicable fee
5. Allow 4–8 weeks for processing

## QLeave — Portable Long Service Leave

QLeave is a mandatory portable long service leave scheme for the Queensland building and construction industry. If you employ workers on building sites in Queensland, you must register as an employer with QLeave and pay the levy.

### Key obligations:

- **Register as an employer** — Any business engaging workers on Queensland building work must register with QLeave.
- **Quarterly levy payments** — The current levy rate is 2.5% of ordinary time earnings for all eligible workers, paid quarterly.
- **Worker registration** — All eligible workers must be registered individually with QLeave. Workers accumulate entitlements that are portable across employers.

The QLeave levy catches new electrical businesses off guard regularly. It's not optional, and failing to pay triggers penalties and interest that compound quickly. ## Licence Renewal

Both the ESQ contractor licence and the QBCC licence require renewal. Missing either creates a gap where you cannot legally quote or carry out work.

### Electrical Safety Queensland renewal:
- Renew online via the ESQ portal
- Confirm insurance details are current
- Pay renewal fee (check current fee on the ESQ website — fees are updated annually)

### QBCC renewal:
- Submit updated financial information (MFR report or self-declaration)
- Confirm nominated Qualified Supervisor details
- Pay renewal fee based on licence category and revenue band

Build renewal dates into a calendar reminder system. ## Common Compliance Issues for Queensland Electrical Businesses

**1. Holding one licence but not the other**
The ESQ contractor licence and QBCC licence are independent requirements. For solar and most installation work over $3,300, you need both. Audits by QBCC regularly catch businesses with only one.

**2. Missing MFR filing deadlines**
QBCC annual financial reporting is mandatory. Missed deadlines result in automatic suspension — often triggered without warning.

**3. Not registering with QLeave**
Many small electrical businesses don't realise QLeave applies from the first employee. Back-payments plus interest can be significant when discovered at audit.

**4. Nominated Qualified Supervisor not meeting requirements**
If your nominated QS leaves or their licence lapses, your QBCC licence may be immediately at risk. You have a limited window to nominate a replacement — act fast.

**5. Inadequate or lapsed insurance**
Both agencies have minimum insurance requirements. A lapsed policy or reduced coverage level puts your licence in breach. Review [electrical contractor insurance requirements](/blog/electrical-contractor-insurance-australia-2026) annually alongside your policy renewal.

## Solar-Specific Considerations in Queensland

For solar and battery businesses operating in Queensland, additional obligations apply beyond the contractor licences:

- **CEC Accreditation** — Required to design and install solar PV systems eligible for STC rebates under the federal SRES. Issued by the Clean Energy Council, not the state. For a full breakdown of CER audit requirements and what's checked, see our [CER audit prep guide](/blog/cer-audit-prep-solar-installers).
- **Network connection approvals** — Energex and Ergon (now Energy Queensland) have their own connection approval processes for grid-tied solar systems. The [CSIP-Aus export limit requirements](/blog/csip-aus-export-limit-1-5kw-solar) are particularly relevant for Queensland solar in 2026.
- **STC compliance** — Understand the [STC claim process](/blog/stc-claim-process-solar-installers-australia) for Queensland Zone 3 installations and ensure your documentation supports every lodgement.

If you're operating across state borders — northern NSW installs from a Queensland base, for example — you'll need to comply with both state regimes simultaneously. The [NSW CCEW compliance guide](/blog/ccew-nsw-electrical-compliance-guide-2026) covers what's required on the NSW side.
